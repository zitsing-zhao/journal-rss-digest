    ABSTRACT Economic crises may alter not only employees' commitment levels but also the mechanisms through which commitment develops. This study examines whether the determinants of organizational commitment changed following economic crisis in Japanese subsidiaries operating in China and whether such changes differed by employee position. Using repeated employee survey data collected between 2007 and 2011, Study 1 compared temporal changes across four subsidiaries. The study contributes to HRM research by demonstrating that commitment determinants are dynamic, context dependent, and heterogeneous across organizational settings.

Because of the clear benefits of digital experimentation, increases in the number of experiments have strained the resource of online participants/customers. In tension with this scarcity, many digital experiments are not carefully powered for their objectives, leading to either over-sampling that wastes resources or under-sampling that weakens inference. Our method can be implemented in an experimentation pipeline, facilitated with an R package we provide.
