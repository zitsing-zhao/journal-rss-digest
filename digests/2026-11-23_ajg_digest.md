    Abstract How does the language of a charitable appeal, whether communicated in a potential donor’s native or non-native (but proficient) language, influence monetary donations? Whereas research finds that people behave more morally in their native (vs. non-native) language, eight preregistered experiments (N = 7,447) and one archival analysis (N = 3,142) find that people donate less in response to appeals in their native language.
