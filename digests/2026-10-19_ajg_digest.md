    This study examines how telehealth delivery models are associated with access to care and protocol adherence in underserved populations. While prior research reports mixed evidence on telehealth’s effectiveness, we argue that these inconsistencies arise because telehealth is not a single intervention but is implemented through distinct service configurations that address different barriers. Drawing on the theoretical framework of digital economics, we conceptualize telehealth as a technology-mediated service that can alter key economic frictions, including search and verification, transportation, tracking, and setup costs.
