  - DOI: 10.1057/s41267-026-00911-5
  - Authors: Xiaolong Shui; Luyang Chen; Michael A. Witt
  - Published: 2026-10-21
  - Crossref journal: Journal of International Business Studies
  - Abstract:
    Abstract We argue that pre-existing organizational ties can partially buffer against the adverse effects of populist deinstitutionalization. In contrast with the chronic institutional weakness emphasized in prior literature, populist deinstitutionalization reflects a politically driven weakening of institutions that firms had previously relied on. This disrupts established routines and increases the transaction costs of cross-border exchange. For SMEs, which are highly dependent on external institutional support for internationalization, pre-existing ties to government actors, international suppliers, and foreign owners can provide partial buffering by replacing some of the coordination, information, and enforcement functions lost through institutional erosion. Our findings extend institution-based research by identifying politically driven institutional erosion as a distinct challenge for international business and by showing that organizational ties can function as selective, imperfect buffers rather than substitutes for formal institutions.

Addressing potential critiques, the paper emphasizes the framework’s adaptability to resistance and its applicability to diverse organizational contexts. This conceptualization encourages intellectual plurality, fostering historically and conceptually informed insight into politics as a set of power-related organizational and institutional practices.
