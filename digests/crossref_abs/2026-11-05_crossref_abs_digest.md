    ABSTRACT We examine whether legal protection for civic participation affects corporate pollution. Polluters may deter public discipline through strategic lawsuits. Anti‐SLAPP (Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation) statutes mitigate this threat by enabling prompt dismissal and recovery of legal costs. Using a difference‐in‐differences design, we find that enacting an anti‐SLAPP statute in a state significantly reduces toxic emissions from that state by increasing local public pressure.     ABSTRACT Is agricultural productivity conducive to economic development? We develop a two‐country open‐economy Schumpeterian growth model with endogenous takeoff. With both agricultural trade and a subsistence requirement, higher domestic agricultural productivity has ambiguous effects on the economy's takeoff and its transitional growth rate if domestic and imported agricultural goods are substitutes. Without the subsistence requirement, higher domestic agricultural productivity delays industrialization and lowers transitional growth by increasing domestic demand for agricultural labor.     Abstract Inferring the infinitesimal rates of continuous-time Markov chains (CTMCs) is a central challenge in many scientific domains.     ABSTRACT Nail salon workers are acutely and chronically exposed to mixtures of volatile organic compounds (VOCs), yet health risks in New York City (NYC) salons have not been systematically assessed.     ABSTRACT As climate change intensifies flood risks, strengthening individual preparedness is essential for sustainable societies. This study tested whether first‐person three‐dimensional computer graphics (3DCG) flood videos can complement official flood maps in motivating preparedness. We used these materials as operationalizations of experiential and descriptive information, respectively. In a preregistered web experiment with 1000 participants, those who viewed flood videos showed a small but statistically significant increase in preparedness intentions compared with those who viewed flood maps. Mediation analyses showed that, among the measured psychological variables, only fear had a significant indirect effect, suggesting that stimulus‐evoked fear helped explain this increase. We also conducted a functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) study with 45 participants using a within‐subjects design. Flood videos elicited greater right amygdala activation than flood maps, consistent with fear‐related processing. Together, these findings provide convergent behavioral and neural evidence that first‐person 3DCG flood videos evoke stronger fear‐related responses than flood maps and that subjective fear helps account for their effect on preparedness intentions. The results suggest that visually vivid flood videos may complement map‐based communication by increasing emotional salience and promoting flood‐preparedness intentions.
