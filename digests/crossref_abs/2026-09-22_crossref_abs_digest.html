This study provides a rigorous empirical test for motivated memory as a cause for procrastination. In a longitudinal experiment over four weeks, individuals had to complete a cumbersome task of unknown length and receive noisy signals about their assigned workload. Varying scope for motivated memory results in more optimistic beliefs among workers who receive negative signals but not among those who receive positive ones. This suppression of negative news through motivated memory causes individuals to defer more work to the future. Abstract We compare and combine two distinct approaches to estimating market power in the US brewing industry—one based on production data, the other on demand. Knowledge diffusion, rising inequality, and the dynamics of a pulled front
2026-09-21 | DOI: 10.1093/ej/ueag119 | The Economic Journal
Authors: Mark Staley
Affiliations: King’s Hill Research , 120 Adelaide Street West, Suite 2500, Toronto, Ontario M5H 1T1
Abstract A model of economic growth is presented in which workers increase their labour income by learning from higher-paid workers. They also experience individual random income shocks. A calibration of the model to U.S. wage micro data implies that these shocks are Brownian ‘noise’, with drift close to zero. Growth comes from occasional random advances at the frontier, which create novel learning opportunities for others and drive sustained growth. The long-run growth rate is increasing in both the variance of shocks and the knowledge-diffusion rate. The model closely reproduces the evolving shape of the U.S. wage distribution between 1991 and 2023.

Authors: Zvi Eckstein; Michael Keane; Osnat Lifshitz
Affiliations: Reichman University Herzliya Israel; Johns Hopkins University Baltimore Maryland USA; University of New South Wales Sydney Australia
ABSTRACT We study why US college graduation gaps by gender and race widened across cohorts. We develop a dynamic life‐cycle model with endogenous education, labor supply, marriage, fertility, and welfare participation. Preferences are fixed across cohorts and race/ethnic groups; changes arise from family background, labor‐market opportunities, and marriage markets. Labor‐market changes account for half of growth in women's graduation from the 1960 to 1980 cohorts. Parental background accounts for one quarter and marriage markets for one fifth. Authors: Jun Ma; Vadim Marmer; Pai Xu
Affiliations: School of Economics Renmin University of China Beijing China; Vancouver School of Economics University of British Columbia Vancouver Canada; Business School University of Hong Kong Hong Kong China
ABSTRACT This paper examines bid requirements, where the government may cancel a procurement contract unless two or more bids are received. Using a first‐price auction model with endogenous entry, we compare the bid requirement and reserve price mechanisms in terms of auction failure and procurement costs. We find that, in comparison with bid requirements, reserve prices can reduce procurement costs and substantially lower failure probabilities, especially when entry costs are high or signals are sufficiently informative. Bid requirements are more likely to result in zero entry, while reserve prices can sustain positive entry under broader conditions.

Abstract Siddique et al. (2024) report large effects of a mobile phone-based health awareness campaign in a field experiment conducted in rural Bangladesh and India during the COVID-19 pandemic. Treatments are stated as being randomized at the village level in the Bangladesh arm, but analyses of available replication materials from this and related field experiments show that this was not the case: participants retained their treatment group assignment from two earlier studies run by the same NGO. Because these earlier studies reported large and persistent treatment effects, the treatment groups in Siddique et al. (2024) could plausibly differ systematically, raising issues of confounding and interaction effects that invalidate the analyses and interpretations of this study, affecting both internal and external validity.
