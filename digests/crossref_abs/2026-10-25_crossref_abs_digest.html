Authors: Homer Wagenaar; Christopher L. Colvin
Affiliations: Belgian Competition Authority Brussels Belgium; Queen's University Belfast Belfast UK
Abstract We study the patent system of the United Kingdom of the Netherlands (1815–30), a post‐Napoleonic state which brought together an early industrializing region that subsequently became Belgium with a later‐industrializing one comprising what is now the Netherlands. The system formally distinguished between patents of invention and patents of importation, protecting both domestic inventions and the introduction of foreign technologies. It also granted administrators considerable discretion over patent fees, terms, and conditions.
