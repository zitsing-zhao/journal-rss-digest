  - Abstract:
    A central tenet of many leadership theories is that leaders treat their followers differently. Although leader differentiation is ubiquitous, its effects on leaders and followers are not well understood and theory and research have not simultaneously considered its three key properties: Leader–member exchange (LMX) differentiation, LMX quality, and LMX social comparison. We developed a theoretical model in which these three properties interact to influence followers’ supervisory interactional justice perceptions and subsequently their supervisor-directed deviance and supervisor-directed organizational citizenship behaviors (OCBs). To test our model, we conducted a pre-registered experiment (Study 1), a single-level, multi-wave study (Study 2), and a multilevel, multi-wave, multisource study (Study 3). Third, when LMX quality and LMX social comparison were both high, LMX differentiation’s positive indirect effect on supervisor-directed deviance, and its negative indirect effect on supervisor-directed OCBs, via supervisory interactional justice perceptions, were the weakest and not reliably different from zero. These are among the first studies to simultaneously theorize and investigate the interactive effects of the three central properties of leader differentiation, and to demonstrate its effects for both followers and leaders.

  - Abstract:
    As organizations mature, standardized systems help meet increasing needs for efficiency. However, this often results in resources being used in rigid ways, compromising the flexibility necessary to leverage opportunities, create value, and address customers’ emerging needs. Here we address solutions to this drift to rigidity, focusing on how operational managers bundle resources to create value for the firm. Our qualitative study is based on data collected from an organization with operations across 252 locations. We extend earlier work by specifying how a particular type of resource orchestration—ORB—constitutes a value-generating micro-mechanism of dynamic capabilities. ORB uncovers the role of operational managers in balancing standardization versus customization through identifying and combining resources.

    The design of online algorithms for matching markets and revenue management settings is usually bound by the assumption that the demand process is formed by a fixed-length sequence of queries with unknown types, each drawn independently. This notion of serial independence implies that the demand of each type, that is, the number of queries of a given type, has low variance and is approximately Poisson distributed. This paper proposes a nonparametric framework for modeling arrival sequences in online stochastic matching that departs from the serial independent assumption.   - Abstract:
    Abstract Between the seventeenth and twentieth centuries, billions of cowries arrived in West Africa, leaving one of the few quantitative traces available for historians to measure the Atlantic trade's impact on the precolonial market economy. Yet, current estimates substantially underestimate the volume of cowry imports. Drawing on unexplored and underutilized sources, most notably French records, this article presents a new annual time series of cowry imports into West Africa between 1650 and 1905. This series reframes three long‐standing debates. First, contrary to arguments that the slave trade's negative economic impacts were already evident in its early phases, cowry imports grew as the slave trade progressed. Second, by reconstructing the nominal cowry stock, the article shows that the end of the slave trade in a context without a clear alternative caused a lasting contraction of the market economy. Third, the interplay between cowry and coin imports challenges the influential ‘great inflation’ thesis by showing that the region absorbed much larger currency inflows than previously assumed. Together, these findings reveal a highly responsive but exposed monetary system that alternated between rapid growth and deep depressions, a pattern that shaped the Atlantic trade's long‐run economic impact in West Africa.
