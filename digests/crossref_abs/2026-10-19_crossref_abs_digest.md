- [Action-oriented Narratives. How Innovation Emerges from Crowd Discussions](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2024.19136)
  - DOI: 10.1287/orsc.2024.19136
  - Authors: Claudio Biscaro; Arvind Malhotra; Ann Majchrzak
  - Affiliations: Johannes Kepler Universität Linz, Institute of Leadership and Change Management, Linz, Austria; UNC at Chapel Hill, Kenan-Flagler Business School, Chapel Hill, North Carolina, United States; University of Southern California, Information Systems, Los Angeles, California, United States
  - Published: 2026-10-19
  - Crossref journal: Organization Science
  - Abstract:
    Crowds are increasingly invited to tackle complex innovation problems. However, our understanding of how crowds solve these problems has limited our ability to utilize them effectively. In this article, we examine how a crowd tasked with finding ways to preserve New Zealand’s native flora and fauna achieves solutions to this complex innovation problem through discussion. Our analysis reveals that discussions leading to innovative solutions differ from those leading to non-innovative solutions: innovation-yielding discussions (1) mobilize more Resources than Constraints, (2) converge on a single Goal, and (3) specify few (often one) responsible Subject(s); vis-à-vis, discussions leading to non-innovative solutions often surfaced multiple goals—thereby sustaining multiple problem framings—while subjects remained vague, implicit, or multiplied, and constraints frequently outnumbered resources. Moreover, we show how the specific weavings of these discursive elements support the crowd’s navigation of the complex innovation problem. Three discursive maneuvers – subject-goal anchoring, repurposing distant knowledge, and layering resources – are fundamental navigational tools used by the crowd to form what we call ‘action-oriented narratives:’ simple, dynamic, and evolving linguistic structures that enable collective problem exploration while giving room for localized improvisation. We contribute to the literature on problem solving by showing how specific linguistic activities alter individuals’ capacity to contribute to and solve complex problems. We also contribute to the literature on linguistic structures supporting innovation by showing that the types of discussions that support innovation depend on the openness of the setting.

  - Authors: TEJAS PARASHER
  - Affiliations: University of California
  - Published: 2026-10-19
  - Crossref journal: American Political Science Review
  - Abstract:
    This article reconsiders the relationship between the globalization of political liberalism and the globalization of political equality. Departing from interpretations of liberal theories of rights and representation as egalitarian or democratic, the article reconstructs an elitist paradigm of liberal representative government from the colonial world. It does so by offering a new reading of the thought of Indian political leader Dadabhai Naoroji. It recovers Naoroji’s overlooked commentary on political representation and imperial reform. I show that Naoroji drew upon analyses made by the first generation of Indian liberal thinkers about imperial political economy and mass-elite relations and used these arguments to formulate a distinctive model of electoral politics as a form of hierarchical tutelage. The recovery of Naoroji’s theory illustrates the deep tensions between representation and mass politics that marked liberalism’s global circulation.

- [TRUE AND PSEUDO-TRUE PARAMETERS](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0266466626100656)
  - DOI: 10.1017/s0266466626100656
  - Authors: Isaiah Andrews; Harvey Barnhard; Jacob Carlson
  - Affiliations: MIT and NBER; Harvard University
  - Published: 2026-10-19
  - Crossref journal: Econometric Theory
  - Abstract:
    Parameter estimates in misspecified models converge to pseudo-true parameter values, which minimize a population objective function. Pseudo-true values often differ from quantities of economic interest, raising questions of how, if at all, they are relevant for decision-making. To study this question, we consider Bayesian decision-makers facing a linear population minimum distance problem. Within a class of priors motivated by the minimum distance objective, we characterize prior sequences under which posteriors concentrate on the pseudo-true value. This convergence is fragile to small changes in priors, implying that pseudo-true values are relevant for decision-making only in special cases. Constructive results are nevertheless possible in this setting, and we derive simple confidence intervals that guarantee correct average coverage for the true parameter under every prior in the class we study, with no bound on the magnitude of misspecification.

  - Authors: Marcus J. Chambers
  - Affiliations: University of Essex
  - Published: 2026-10-19
  - Crossref journal: Econometric Theory
  - Abstract:
    Phillips and Solo (1992, Annals of Statistics 20, 971–2001) provided sets of conditions concerning the summability of coefficients and the properties of innovations in discrete-time linear processes such that normalized sums satisfy certain asymptotic properties, such as laws of large numbers, central limit theorems, and invariance principles. In this article, we consider ARMA processes in continuous time that are sampled at discrete-time intervals and provide conditions on the continuous-time process such that the results of Phillips and Solo can be utilized for normalized sums of the discrete-time observations. We utilize a state-space representation in the case of stock and flow variables, both of which are a linear combination of the state vector, and derive the relevant asymptotics based on multivariate extensions of the Phillips–Solo results. An illustrative application of the results to unit (zero) root testing is provided.

  - Published: 2026-10-19
  - Crossref journal: International Economic Review
  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT This paper investigates how economic catch‐up shapes resource allocation in emerging economies. I develop a model that features firm‐specific technology adoption and costly adjustment of production factors. Rapid technological progress during catch‐up creates strong demand for reallocating resources from slow to fast adopters, amplifying the consequences of adjustment costs. The amplification fades as technological change gradually slows during convergence to a more advanced and efficient steady state. I evaluate the model using Chinese and German enterprise data and find that differences in development stage account for at least 40% of their labour allocative efficiency gap.

- [EXPRESS: Improving Operational Performance through Boarding Time Slack: Evidence from the U.S. Airline Industry](https://doi.org/10.1177/10591478261500799)
  - DOI: 10.1177/10591478261500799
  - Authors: Chandrasekhar Manchiraju; Ashutosh Bhave; Milind G. Sohoni
  - Published: 2026-10-19
  - Crossref journal: Production and Operations Management
  - Abstract:
    Flight delays have a significant impact on airlines, passengers, and the U.S. economy. Airlines also use flight on-time performance (OTP) – a metric based on the proportion of delayed flights – to attract travelers by demonstrating service reliability. It is well established that adding buffers to flight time and ground time improves on-time arrivals. However, such buffers can impose significant costs. The flight and ground times comprise multiple activities, and prior research has primarily focused on studying the impact of aggregate buffers. In this paper, we study the impact of increasing the buffer of a critical ground time activity, namely boarding time, potentially without increasing the aggregate ground time buffer, on flight delays. To do so, we use the context of Delta Air Lines' recent policy changes wherein the airline increased the boarding time for domestic narrow-body flights by 5 minutes and started compensating flight attendants for pre-flight boarding. Using a Difference-in-Difference-in-Differences framework, we analyze and quantify the impact of these policies on Delta’s flight delays. Our findings indicate a significant reduction in departure delays following the policy changes, with increased boarding time playing a critical role in improving on-time departures. Our analysis finds no evidence that boarding compensation contributed to the improvement in on-time departures. We show that the increase in boarding time can improve the likelihood of on-time departures, potentially without requiring additional total ground time or incurring additional scheduling costs. We observe that the benefit is more pronounced for longer flights, busier routes, and later departures in the day. Airlines are often criticized for relying on costly, ad hoc schedule buffering to improve OTP. However, our results demonstrate that carefully planning buffer time for critical activities can significantly improve operational performance.

- [EXPRESS: Assortment Optimization Given Basket Shopping Behavior Using the Ising Model](https://doi.org/10.1177/10591478261502217)
  - DOI: 10.1177/10591478261502217
  - Authors: Andrey Vasilyev; Sebastian Maier; Ralf W. Seifert
  - Published: 2026-10-18
  - Crossref journal: Production and Operations Management
  - Abstract:
    In markets where customers tend to purchase baskets of products rather than single products, assortment optimization is a major challenge for retailers. Removing a product from a retailer's assortment can result in a severe drop in aggregate demand if this product is a complement to other products. Therefore, accounting for the complementarity effect is essential when making assortment decisions. In this paper, we develop an analytical framework designed to address this problem. We model customers' choices using the binary Ising model, which is a special case of a Markov random field. This model captures pairwise demand dependencies as well as the individual attractiveness of each product, with binary variables indicating whether products are included in the purchased basket. For estimation, we utilize existing methods developed for the spin formulation of the Ising model and then apply a parameter transformation to express the fitted model in the binary form used for assortment optimization. We show that the assortment optimization problem under the Ising model is NP-hard. We also provide multiple theoretical insights into the structure of the optimal assortments based on the graphical representation of the Ising model, and propose several heuristic algorithms that can be used to obtain high-quality solutions to the assortment optimization problem. Our numerical analysis demonstrates that the developed simulated annealing procedure leads to an expected profit gain of 15% compared to offering an unoptimized assortment (where all products are included) and around 5% compared to using a revenue-ordered heuristic algorithm.
