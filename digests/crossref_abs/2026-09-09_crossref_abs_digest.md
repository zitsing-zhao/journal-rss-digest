  - DOI: 10.1111/ehr.70142
  - Authors: Oscar Gelderblom; Abe de Jong; Joost Jonker
  - Affiliations: Faculty of Arts, Department of History University of Antwerp Antwerp Belgium; Faculty of Economics and Business, Department of Economics, Econometrics, and Finance University of Groningen Groningen the Netherlands; Monash Business School, Department of Banking and Finance Monash University Melbourne Australia; Faculty of Humanities, Department of History University of Amsterdam Amsterdam the Netherlands
  - Published: 2026-09-08
  - Crossref journal: The Economic History Review
  - Abstract:
    Abstract In 1609, the Dutch East India Company (VOC) faced a bear raid by a syndicate of short sellers led by Isaac Le Maire, a former director. We reconstruct the forward trade between 1607 and 1611, the actions taken by the syndicate members, and the responses to these actions by the company. We argue that the bear syndicate members aimed for financial gain from share price declines, while their leader, Le Maire, participated for strategic reasons, that is, breaking up the VOC monopoly. We also show that the government refused to intervene at that time. The raid ended with a squeeze executed by an opposing syndicate, bankrupting several forward traders and blocking Le Maire's plans. We characterize the bear raid and squeeze as a crucial episode in the more general tug‐of‐war between company directors aiming to turn the VOC into an instrument of the state and a diverse group of opposing investors.

  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT Open source engagement is an ongoing effort in which members continuously interact with evolving project dynamics. Engagement extends beyond writing code or setting policy; it is a shared practice of understanding, negotiating, and adapting to the social and technical structures that define a project. As members continually evaluate their project engagement, collective action threats may arise that impact the overall functioning and sustainability of a project.   - Abstract:
    Purpose The study aims to examine how firms in a supply network (SN) dependent on critical raw materials (CRMs) adapt to environmental uncertainty arising from contemporary geopolitical tensions. To address this aim, we draw on the theoretical lenses of adaptive cycles of the panarchy theory and resource dependence theory. Design/methodology/approach We employ a sequence-structuring theoretical elaboration approach, treating adaptation as an evolving process, to investigate adaptive responses to geopolitical tensions within CRM-dependent SN.   - Authors: Massimo G. Colombo; Annalisa Croce; Elisa Ughetto
  - Affiliations: Politecnico di Milano; Politecnico di Torino
  - Published: 2026-09-08
  - Crossref journal: Journal of Management Studies
  - Abstract:
    Abstract We examine how the two dimensions of VC managers' personal reputation, that is, ‘being known’ and ‘being known for quality’, jointly influence the formation of initial syndication ties. We propose that VC managers follow a two‐stage cognitive process. In the first stage, the ‘being known for quality’ dimension – a reflection of their abilities‐must enter their ‘consideration set’. The ‘being known’ dimension is crucial to draw attention to potential candidates. In the second stage, lead VC managers evaluate the signal conveyed by the ‘being known for quality’ dimension of prospective partners' reputation. This effect is stronger if they share a previous common employer, have a similar industrial or geographic investment focus, or work at VC firms that have previously syndicated one or more investments with each other or with the same VC firm.

  - Affiliations: MIT ,
  - Published: 2026-09-08
  - Crossref journal: Journal of the European Economic Association
  - Abstract:
    Abstract We model the interplay of employment relations and community-based interactions among workers and managers. Employment relations can be either tough (where workers are monitored intensively and obtain few rents, and managers do not provide informal favors for workers) or soft (where there is less monitoring, more worker rents, and more workplace favor exchange). Both workers and managers also exert effort in providing community public goods. The threat of losing access to public goods can motivate managers to keep employment soft; conversely, the threat of losing future employment or future workers’ trust can motivate workers and managers to provide effort in the community. Corporate tax cuts; improved worker monitoring; automation, outsourcing, and offshoring; declines in the minimum wage; and opportunities for residential segregation or for privatizing community-provided services can make both workers and managers worse-off by undermining soft employment relations and community cooperation.

- [Beyond stereotypes: Examining the social backgrounds and life trajectories of women in sex work](https://doi.org/10.1177/00380385261452994)

  - Affiliations: The Danish Center for Social Science Research, Copenhagen, Denmark
  - Published: 2026-09-08
  - Crossref journal: Sociology
  - Abstract:
    The question of who engages in sex work continues to attract attention. While recent research emphasises the heterogeneity of people who sell sex, little is known about how sex workers’ social backgrounds and life trajectories compare with those of their non-sex-worker peers. Applying a life-course perspective, we combine police records and social service registers with administrative data from Statistics Denmark to analyse the social backgrounds and life trajectories of 2478 female sex workers. Using latent class models, we assess social problems across childhood, adolescence and adulthood. Results reveal substantial heterogeneity, with most sex workers experiencing few problems while a small subgroup face elevated risks of mental health issues, crime, victimisation, substance misuse and homelessness. Analyses show that those with the most complex problems often come from disadvantaged families. Comparisons with two control groups highlight both similarities in and differences between the life trajectories of sex workers and other women.
