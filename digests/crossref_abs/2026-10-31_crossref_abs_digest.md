Managerial Summary We examine how peer activity shapes where multinational headquarters focus their attention during foreign operations. Rather than monitoring every foreign subsidiary equally, headquarters rely on cues from peers to decide where to look more closely, but not all peer cues are equally influential. Among peers outside the firm, industry competitors have a stronger pull on headquarter attention than firms from the same home country, meaning competitive dynamics matter more than home‐country familiarity in directing headquarter attention. Within the firm, geographic regional groupings shape attention more powerfully than broad market categorizations. When attention is directed toward one subsidiary, it spills over to other subsidiaries in the same grouping, amplifying their visibility. Managers can anticipate where attention flows by understanding which peer categories headquarters finds most compelling.
