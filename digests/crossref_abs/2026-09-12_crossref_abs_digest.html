Sabotage as Industrial Policy
2026-09-11 | DOI: 10.1093/ej/ueag111 | The Economic Journal
Authors: Jin Liu; Martin Rotemberg; Sharon Traiberman
Affiliations: UPenn; NYU; NYU & NBER
Abstract We characterise sabotage, exemplified by recent US policies concerning China’s semiconductor industry, as trade policy. For some (but not all) goods, completely destroying foreigners’ productivity increases domestic real income by shifting the location of production and improving the terms of trade. The gross benefit of sabotage can be summarised by a few sufficient statistics: trade and demand elasticities and import and production shares. The cost of sabotage is determined by countries’ relative unit labour costs for the sabotaged goods. We find important non-monotonicities: for semiconductors, partially sabotaging foreign production would lower US real income, while comprehensive sabotage would raise it.

ABSTRACT The 2018 China Initiative by the Trump administration complicated procedures and reduced funding for U.S.‐China research collaborations. Using Scopus data, we analyze its impact on Chinese research. We find that it significantly lowered the quality of both publications and co‐authors of Chinese researchers with prior U.S. collaborations compared to those with prior European collaborations, and reduced yearly citations for affected researchers by 6%. The effect was stronger for high‐productivity Chinese researchers in U.S.‐dominated fields. While China is at the forefront of the scientific frontier, it still depends more on the U.S. than the reverse.

ABSTRACT The paper shows that population aging can reduce the welfare cost to individuals of a persistent negative local shock, because slower out‐migration restrains forward‐looking capital disinvestment and the retained capital cushions the wages of those who stay.
