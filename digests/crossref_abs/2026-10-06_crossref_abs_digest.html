Union Strength, Collective Bargaining, and Strike Activity: Insights From Chile's 2017 Labor Reform
2026-10-06 | DOI: 10.1111/irel.70041 | Industrial Relations: A Journal of Economy and Society
Authors: Martín Dibarrart; Esteban Puentes; Tomás Rau
Affiliations: University of Minnesota Minneapolis Minnesota USA; Department of Economics Universidad de Chile Santiago Chile; Instituto de Economía Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile and IZA Santiago Chile
ABSTRACT We study the causal impact of Chile's 2017 labor reform—which banned the replacement of striking workers and restricted unilateral extension of collective benefits—on strike activity, wages, and employment. Using administrative data on all regulated collective negotiations from 2008 to 2024 in a difference‐in‐differences framework that exploits pre‐reform variation in union density, we find no increase in strike incidence and modest decreases in strike voting and actual stoppages among more unionized firms. Effects on wages are negligible; effects on employment are positive but display pre‐reform trends. The pattern is consistent with the joint cost model and with the institutional‐context literature.
