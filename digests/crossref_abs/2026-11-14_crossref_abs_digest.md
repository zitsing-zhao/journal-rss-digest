  - Abstract:
    Service systems in which demand, congestion, and worker incentives evolve jointly are ubiquitous in modern operations. We study a dynamic principal–agent problem in a queueing system with customer abandonment, where the principal controls arrivals through pricing and the agent privately chooses a costly service rate. Modeling workload as a controlled reflected diffusion, we characterize the centralized and decentralized Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations and construct a representative two-part contract consisting of an access charge and a completion payment.     Using a large-scale panel dataset of U.S. buyer firms and their global supplier networks from 2010 to 2020, we examine how global political instability (GPI), a firm’s weighted exposure to political instability across the countries where its international suppliers operate, is associated with firm performance.
