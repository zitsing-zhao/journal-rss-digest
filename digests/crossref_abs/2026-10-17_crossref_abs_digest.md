- [Simulating competitive collocation strategies with deep reinforcement learning](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41267-026-00914-2)
  - DOI: 10.1057/s41267-026-00914-2
  - Authors: Roman Gwosdilin; Thomas Lindner; Michael Wolfesberger
  - Published: 2026-10-16
  - Crossref journal: Journal of International Business Studies
  - Abstract:
    Abstract This paper introduces a simulation model based on deep reinforcement learning to study competitive collocation strategies of two internationalizing firms. Drawing on the strategic interaction and competitive collocation literature in IB, we explore the factors determining the performance-optimizing degree of collocation in market-seeking internationalization for a duopoly in product markets. Our simulation suggests that a high degree of collocation between the two firms is optimal in many circumstances. Whereas mid-to-high levels of collocation are driven by low competitive intensity, low entry barriers, low home-market size, and high market-size variation, complete collocation emerges under high competitive intensity, low first-mover advantage, and smaller home-market size. High entry barriers, combined with low variation in market size and strong liabilities of foreignness, drive the opposite case of complete mutual avoidance. A key takeaway from the simulation is a focus on the competing firm in global expansion: in many cases, it becomes key to limit the rival’s growth options and to enter key markets before access becomes more restricted. We discuss the possibilities of using computer-simulation-based approaches to theorizing on internationalization.

  - Abstract:
    Recent evidence, both in the laboratory and in the wild, suggests that the use of generative artificial intelligence reduces the diversity of content produced. As generative AI penetrates more markets and reaches widespread adoption, should we still expect to see content homogenization? Our work begins with a simple observation: content producers do not act in isolation. Instead, they compete with one another (e.g., for customers or attention). We explore the impact competition has on both content diversity and overall social welfare when producers use generative AI.   - DOI: 10.1002/smj.70136
  - Authors: Shiva Agarwal; Ram Ranganathan
  - Affiliations: The University of Texas at Austin McCombs School of Business Austin Texas USA
  - Published: 2026-10-16
  - Crossref journal: Strategic Management Journal
  - Abstract:
    Abstract Research Summary Digital platforms coordinate complementor innovations through interfaces into their core components. However, when interfaces themselves evolve alongside core component innovations, complementors may exhibit behaviors that are not entirely predictable. Using granular data from Apple's five major iOS releases, we abductively examine App developers’ responses following Apple's core component innovations. We document that, in post‐release periods, complementors simultaneously increase co‐innovation on iOS while being more likely to multihome by diversifying to the competing platform. These choices are further explained by whether architectural changes reflect cross‐platform standardization or platform‐specific differentiation. Standardization spurs both complementor co‐innovation and multihoming, while differentiation reduces diversification tendencies. The patterns that we document reveal an important tension: governing through interfaces enables platforms’ scalability but constrains their ability to control complementors as platforms evolve. Managerial Summary Digital platforms like Apple coordinate large scale innovation through standardized interfaces, but those interfaces must evolve as platforms compete, and the direction of that evolution has consequences managers should attend to. Analyzing Apple's five major iOS releases, we find that architectural changes are associated with increased co‐innovation but also a higher likelihood that developers diversify to a competing platform. This pattern is particularly pronounced when updates bring iOS closer to Android, suggesting that architectural convergence may lower the barriers to cross‐platform expansion for developers. Importantly, this behavior is strongest among highest‐value developers. Platform managers should therefore treat architectural evolution not merely as a technical decision, but as a strategic one with direct implications for ecosystem loyalty, competitive differentiation, and the retention of valuable ecosystem partners.

    ABSTRACT Our paper builds a bridge between the emerging strand of research on public administration under populist rule and the established literature on delegation to independent regulatory authorities (IRAs). We focus on Italy, a critical case in Western Europe, which between 2001 and 2025 experienced six coalition governments dominated or strongly influenced by populist parties of different ideological orientations. Findings highlight that delegation to IRAs is remarkably resilient once institutionalized, even under sustained political contestation.   - Abstract:
    Abstract Predictive algorithms are increasingly used to support frontline decision-making in public administration, but evidence remains limited on how they affect bureaucratic capacity. This study examines county-level adoption of predictive screening tools in U.S. Child Protective Services using NCANDS data (2010–2023) and a stacked difference-in-differences design. Leveraging staggered adoption, I find no change in the total number of investigations opened and a sizable decline (about 15%) in the share of investigations that are substantiated. These results are inconsistent with operational capacity gains and more consistent with misclassification that erodes analytical capacity. These findings are consistent with a mechanism of routinized deference: under workload and accountability pressures, algorithmic recommendations can become procedurally defensible defaults. Overall, this study argues that predictive screening is not capacity-enhancing by default, as routinized deference can undermine the professional judgment central to bureaucratic work.
