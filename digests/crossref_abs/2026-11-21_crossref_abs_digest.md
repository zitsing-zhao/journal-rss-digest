  - DOI: 10.2308/tar-2024-0581
  - Authors: Edmond Tapero; Dan Weiss
  - Affiliations: Tel Aviv University
  - Published: 2026-11-20
  - Crossref journal: The Accounting Review
  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT Although the prevalence of losses has been increasing over the years, little is known about how managerial decisions lead to reversing losses back to profits. We find cost elasticity choices, implied by operating actions made in advance, significantly increase the likelihood of loss reversal. We show loss firms with high cost elasticity exhibit significantly higher long-term survival rates and are therefore less likely to exercise the abandonment option than loss firms with low cost elasticity. The contributions are twofold. First, the findings shed light on a mechanism underlying loss reversal. We extend the loss literature by promoting the understanding of how ex ante cost elasticity choices affect the likelihood of loss reversal. Second, the findings enrich the cost accounting literature by demonstrating a unique performance implication of a cost elasticity choice.   - Published: 2026-11-20
  - Crossref journal: Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice
  - Abstract:
    Is war good or bad for entrepreneurship? Entrepreneurs are often situated in extreme environments characterized by violent conflict. Yet existing entrepreneurship scholarship has largely examined war and conflict only at the micro or meso level, leaving the aggregate, macro-level entrepreneurial consequences of large-scale war underexplored. We provide a synthetic control study of entrepreneurial dynamics in the Russo-Ukrainian conflict during 2009 to 2019 as a first step toward bridging this gap. Relative to the synthetic counterfactual, registered LLCs in Ukraine declined by approximately 13% at the 2016 peak (74,000 missing firms) before recovering toward the synthetic trajectory by 2019. Self-employment in Ukraine dropped by approximately 4 percentage points (pp) at the 2015 peak, corresponding to roughly 680,000 Ukrainians no longer self-employed relative to the counterfactual, with partial recovery to a residual shortfall of approximately 280,000 by 2019. For Russia, the gap is delayed but accumulating, widening from −0.4 pp in 2015 to −1.6 pp by 2019. This corresponds to approximately 1.56 million missing LLCs relative to the counterfactual, a 32% relative loss. The contraction in Russian LLCs accumulates rather than attenuates over time. In Ukraine, we also observe a gender gap in post-2014 self-employment deviations from the synthetic counterfactual, with female self-employment falling more than male self-employment. To interpret these findings, we propose a conceptual framework that combines critical-junctures theory with the literature on entrepreneurship under authoritarian and democratic institutional regimes, arguing that the divergent post-2014 institutional trajectories of Russia and Ukraine help explain the contrasting entrepreneurial patterns we observe. - [Cognitive Ambidexterity: Balancing Algorithmic Efficiency and Contextual Judgement in AI-Enabled Decision Systems](https://doi.org/10.1177/01492063261501111)
  - DOI: 10.1177/01492063261501111
  - Authors: Tahir Abbas Syed
  - Affiliations: University of Manchester
  - Published: 2026-11-21
  - Crossref journal: Journal of Management
  - Abstract:
    AI-enabled decision systems make algorithmic recommendations appear objective and ready for action before contextual, ethical, and stakeholder concerns are fully considered. This study examines how leaders preserve judgment under these conditions. We develop cognitive ambidexterity as a leader-level meta-cognitive capability through which managers balance algorithmic exploitation with contextual exploration in AI-mediated decisions. Using an exploratory sequential mixed-methods design, we first examine five U.K. procurement and infrastructure organizations and identify four mechanisms of cognitive ambidexterity. We then examine these mechanisms through vignette experiments with managers. The findings show that leaders preserve contextual judgment by treating AI outputs as interpretive cues, calibrating trust under ambiguity, slowing decisions when reflection is needed, and building supports that make algorithmic recommendations contestable. The study extends ambidexterity theory by showing how exploration–exploitation tensions are cognitively regulated when exploitation is embedded in algorithmic decision processes. It also uses leadership cognition and Upper Echelons Theory to explain why leaders differ in their capacity to enact this regulation. Together, these insights position cognitive ambidexterity as a critical leader-level capability for using AI’s efficiency gains while preserving the contextual judgment.

- [“Sit Nice!”: Peer Culture, Teacher Capacity, and Joint Socialization Efforts in Preschool](https://doi.org/10.1177/00031224261498245)
  - DOI: 10.1177/00031224261498245
  - Authors: Casey Stockstill
  - Affiliations: Dartmouth College
  - Published: 2026-11-21
  - Crossref journal: American Sociological Review
  - Abstract:
    Preschools instill soft skills that are crucial to children’s life outcomes. As preschool access expands, research and policy often focus on improving teacher quality. This focus downplays how much teachers’ efforts may depend on children’s peers. Drawing on two years of ethnography, I highlight two important aspects of socialization: teachers’ capacity to deliver messages in the moment, and peers’ tendency to support those messages. I compare socialization processes at two race- and class-segregated preschools. Three interrelated classroom conditions—children’s enrollment, attendance, and challenging behaviors—mattered for socialization interactions. Teachers at both preschools pursued socialization goals of instilling safe and formal classroom behaviors. At the affluent, majority-White preschool, teachers had a greater capacity to coach children in the moment, and peers had a culture of supporting teacher messages. At the low-income, racially diverse preschool, children placed the authority to coach classroom behavior in their teachers, who were pressed for time. I argue that socialization agents jointly influence behavior. In segregated preschools, teachers may shape classroom behavior to unequal extents, preventing early learning settings from disrupting future inequalities.

- [Disclosing work-from-home flexibility to compete for talent? Evidence from job postings](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11142-026-10001-4)
  - DOI: 10.1007/s11142-026-10001-4
  - Authors: Charles Ham; Rebecca N. Hann; Wenfeng Wang; Jingwen Yang
  - Published: 2026-11-21
  - Crossref journal: Review of Accounting Studies
  - Abstract:
    Abstract Job postings serve as one of the primary means to communicate with prospective employees, enabling employers to highlight the benefits of the position and attract qualified applicants. As work-from-home flexibility has become increasingly prevalent, this study examines whether employers strategically disclose work-from-home flexibility in their job postings and how this disclosure is associated with hiring outcomes. Using a comprehensive sample of job postings from US employers between 2017 and 2024, we find that employers are more likely to disclose work-from-home flexibility when facing greater labor market competition and that disclosing this flexibility is associated with a higher job vacancy filling rate, especially when work-from-home information is disclosed in a more salient manner. However, we document a positive association between work-from-home disclosures and subsequent employee turnover, suggesting potential costs associated with these disclosures. These findings add to the growing literature on firms’ strategic disclosure targeted towards labor markets.

  - Authors: Dustin Cole; Jacob Jensen; Tobias Schoenherr

  - Published: 2026-11-24
  - Crossref journal: International Journal of Operations &amp; Production Management
  - Abstract:
    Purpose Operations and supply chain managers are continuously pulled between efficiency to reduce costs and buffering for resilience. These endeavors are at the heart of Factory Physics, which theoretically defines the levers managers use to control the physics of an organization. However, organizations are also under pressure to incorporate environmental impacts into their decision-making due to consumer and regulatory pressures. With managerial decisions on inventory turnover and capacity utilization having direct environmental impacts, managers are thus challenged to consider environmental performance alongside economic consequences in an increasingly demanding environment. Taking a Factory Physics lens, this research focuses on the environmental impact of inventory turnover and capacity utilization, while considering potential nonlinearities. Design/methodology/approach A high-dimensional fixed-effects approach is employed with a panel dataset of 4,638 operations and supply chain firms from 2002 to 2026. Findings The results suggest that increasing inventory turnover and capacity utilization improve environmental performance. However, there is a nonlinearity: beyond a certain point, additional inventory turnover no longer improves environmental performance. A similar effect is found for capacity utilization when financial leverage is high. Extending this in a post hoc analysis, the findings suggest that capacity utilization is dominant in reducing environmental harm. Further, increasing inventory turnover only improves environmental performance in later stages of the process. Originality/value This research provides a holistic examination of the environmental implications of Factory Physics, considering the multiple levers managers use to manage Factory Physics and the potential for nonlinear outcomes.

  - DOI: 10.1177/00380385261484085
  - Authors: Ian Brunton-Smith; Patrick Sturgis
  - Affiliations: Department of Sociology, University of Surrey, Guildford, UK; Department of Methodology, The London School of Economics and Political Science, UK
  - Published: 2026-11-20
  - Crossref journal: Sociology
  - Abstract:
    Evidence on the methodological evolution of sociology in the 21st century is limited. Useful signals exist in the literature, but we lack a temporally extended description of how the discipline’s practice has changed across the period in which the data and method environment was most transformed. The constraint is the cost of hand-coding journal articles, capping feasible corpus sizes well below what disciplinary description requires. We use locally hosted large language models (LLMs) to break this constraint, validating their performance before applying the pipeline to nearly 20,000 full-text articles from 28 sociology journals over 2004–2024. We document a steady empirical turn, near-parity between quantitative and qualitative work, and a wholesale shift of survey administration online. These patterns bear on Savage and Burrows’ diagnosis of a ‘coming crisis of empirical sociology’: contrary to their expectations, the survey has held its share and the interview has grown, while big data has remained marginal.
