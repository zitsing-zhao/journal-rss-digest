  - Abstract:
    Occupational gender segregation remains a defining feature of labor markets worldwide, and efforts to recruit individuals into gender-atypical occupations often fail because newcomers drop out shortly after entry. Understanding what drives this early attrition requires examining the day-to-day experiences of newcomers whose gender is underrepresented in their occupation, especially during the critical first weeks of organizational socialization. Integrating role congruity theory with a dynamic work-events perspective, we propose that gender-atypical newcomers experience an intensifying trajectory of perceived negative work events over time, and that this intensifying trajectory undermines their task mastery and heightens their turnover intention.
