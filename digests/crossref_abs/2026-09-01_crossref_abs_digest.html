Affiliations: Queen Mary University of London, TSE, and CEPR London UK; Royal Holloway, University of London Egham Surrey UK; University of Wisconsin–Madison and NBER Madison Wisconsin US
ABSTRACT We develop and estimate a model of endogenous schooling and earnings to isolate the causal effect of parents' education on children's outcomes. Identification uses earnings differences among children with equal schooling whose parents have different schooling. Health and Retirement Study data indicate that the observed intergenerational schooling correlation is largely driven by correlated unobserved preferences for schooling. This is partly offset by a slightly negative structural relationship between parents' and children's schooling choices. Nevertheless, an exogenous one‐year increase in parents' schooling raises children's lifetime earnings by 2 % on average.

The Heterogeneous Impact of Short-Time Work: From Saved Jobs to Windfall Effects,
2026-08-31 | DOI: 10.1093/jeea/jvag052 | Journal of the European Economic Association
Authors: Pierre Cahuc; Francis Kramarz; Sandra Nevoux; Nathan Vieira
Affiliations: Sciences Po and IZA; Innovation Lab at Collẽge de France, Uppsala University; Banque de France; Aix-Marseille University
Abstract This paper investigates the adoption of short-time work by firms and their participating employees. We present a model showing that short-time work can preserve jobs in firms facing severe negative revenue shocks, but not in less affected firms, where hours are reduced without preventing layoffs. Using data from all French establishments during the 2008-2009 Great Recession, we find that short-time work helped save jobs and increased hours worked in firms hit by significant revenue losses, facilitating a faster post-recession recovery. We also identify substantial windfall effects, which raise the policy’s cost per job saved. Nonetheless, short-time work is likely more cost-effective at preserving employment than wage subsidies, potentially resulting in a net negative cost to public finances.
