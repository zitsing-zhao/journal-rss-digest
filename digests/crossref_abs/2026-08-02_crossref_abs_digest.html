Authors: Denise Vesper; Cornelius J. König
Affiliations: Universität des Saarlandes
Strikes are a recurring workplace phenomenon that affects strikers, their families, and managers, but also others such as commuters. To date, studies examining individual-level effects of strikes have been scattered across different fields of study. This systematic literature review summarizes studies on these effects and on how unaffected third parties, such as the public, evaluate strikes. We conducted searches in four databases. The 72 included studies show that strikes can have detrimental effects, for example, on psychological health, but these can be decreased with resources such as social support.
