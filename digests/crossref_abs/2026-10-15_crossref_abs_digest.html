Authors: Friso Selten; Dorus van Loo; Bram Klievink
Affiliations: Institute of Public Administration, Leiden University The Hague the Netherlands
ABSTRACT Advances in digital technologies are expected to fundamentally change how organizations operate and create value. Empirical studies show that public organizations face difficulties organizing and sustaining this digital transformation. To better understand these challenges, this study examines the organizational mechanisms underlying digital transformation in a public organization over the past 20 years. Our findings show that this transformation was primarily enabled by an emergent change approach, with influential planned changes occurring only occasionally. We identify the organizational mechanisms underlying both types of change and analyze their interdependence. Our findings suggest that both emergent and planned change approaches may be needed to enable digital transformation in public organizations effectively, but that combining them is complex in practice.

The Dynamics of the Racial Wealth Gap
2026-10-14 | DOI: 10.1111/iere.70121 | International Economic Review
Authors: Dionissi Aliprantis; Daniel R. Carroll; Eric R. Young
Affiliations: ENS de Lyon Lyon France; Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland Cleveland Ohio USA; University of Virginia Charlottesville Virginia USA
ABSTRACT We study the dynamics of the racial wealth gap (the wealth of Black households relative to white ones) using a dynamic equilibrium model of overlapping generations. We match the model to the cross‐sectional distributions of wealth and earnings by race. We find that equalizing earnings is critical for permanent reductions in racial wealth inequality, and that one‐time transfers will have only transitory effects unless earnings are also equalized. Our results are robust to the introduction of racial differences in returns to saving, which only matter after earnings inequality is reduced.
