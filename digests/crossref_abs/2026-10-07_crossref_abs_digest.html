Targeted Philanthropy: Evidence from M&amp;As
2026-10-07 | DOI: 10.1111/jofi.70090 | The Journal of Finance
Authors: CARA VANSTEENKISTE
ABSTRACT I show how firms optimize the allocation of charitable funds around investments that require external stakeholder support, such as mergers and acquisitions. Acquirers increase donations to target‐related charities before making a takeover offer, but scale funds back after withdrawing from a deal. Firms redistribute funds from nontakeover‐related areas to target insider–affiliated charities, employee communities, and counties with a regulatory presence. Targeted donations that encourage blockholder reciprocity and employee commitment are associated with better deal performance through decreased merger premiums and lower integration costs. Overall, the results highlight firms’ use of philanthropy to manage stakeholder relations in takeover decisions.

Abstract Using résumé data on over 20 million U.S. workers, we find that the flow of employees between a pair of firms sharply drops by about 20% during periods in which the firms share a board director. Affiliations: Payments and Regulatory Oversight, Bank of Canada, Ottawa, Ontario K1A 0G9, Canada; Department of Economics, University of Bern, 3012 Bern, Switzerland; and Study Center Gerzensee, 3115 Gerzensee, Switzerland
Stablecoins are the current go-to digital money to settle transactions on the blockchain. We explore the implications of introducing competing tokenized public digital monies (i.e., tokenized central bank digital currencies (CBDCs)). When they pay a moderate interest rate and guarantee users’ anonymity, tokenized CBDCs crowd out stablecoins. However, when they pay a high interest rate and have low anonymity features, stablecoin issuers could use tokenized CBDCs as collateral, promoting the creation of stablecoins. We identify conditions under which introducing a CBDC to compete with stablecoins in the crypto sector would be socially optimal. Macro‐Economic Uncertainty and Business Cycle Asymmetry
2026-10-06 | DOI: 10.1111/jmcb.70094 | Journal of Money, Credit and Banking
Authors: YU‐FAN HUANG; SUI LUO; HUNG‐JEN WANG
Abstract We propose a modified unobserved‐component model to investigate the link between macro‐economic uncertainty and business cycle asymmetry of the U.S. economy. The degree of skewness of shocks is assumed to vary with macro‐economic uncertainty. We develop an efficient Bayesian Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm. Our estimates suggest that high macro‐economic uncertainty tends to be associated with large and negative shocks to the cyclical (but not the trend) component. Quantitatively, a one‐standard‐deviation increase (decrease) in uncertainty results in an expected output change of −0.15 (0.10) percentage points. Low quantiles of the growth distribution, that is, growth‐at‐risk, are particularly susceptible to changes in uncertainty.
