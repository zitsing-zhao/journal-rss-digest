  - Abstract:
    This paper revisits the exchange rate predictability of commodity currencies through the lens of market efficiency. Focusing on currencies with strong contemporaneous exposure to commodity prices, we show that changes in country-specific commodity export or import prices predict exchange rate fluctuations both in sample and out of sample. We establish four empirical facts: (i) the sign of the predictive relationship runs counter to standard risk-based explanations; (ii) predictability is concentrated at short horizons; (iii) predictability is driven by a small set of thinly traded currencies; and (iv) even sophisticated speculative traders adjust positions sluggishly in response to commodity price shocks in these currencies. - [Bargaining and Contract Forms in Distribution Channels: Why Do Slotting Fees Arise and Linear Contracts Persist?](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2025.03426)
  - DOI: 10.1287/mnsc.2025.03426
  - Authors: Øystein Foros; Hans Jarle Kind; Greg Shaffer
  - Affiliations: Norwegian School of Economics (NHH), 5045 Bergen, Norway; Norwegian School of Economics (NHH), 5045 Bergen, Norway; and CESifo, 81679 Munich, Germany; University of Rochester, Rochester, New York 14627
  - Published: 2026-10-23
  - Crossref journal: Management Science
  - Abstract:
    Two empirical patterns emerge in business-to-business settings, particularly in retail distribution channels such as grocery markets. First, linear contracts are widely observed alongside nonlinear contracts with fixed payments, despite the claimed efficiency advantages of the latter. Second, when fixed payments arise, they often flow from suppliers to retailers (slotting fees) rather than the reverse. We develop and analyze a model of multilateral bargaining in which a supplier negotiates separately with competing retailers to explain these patterns. The key mechanism is a trade-off between two distortions: under linear contracts, wholesale prices reflect bargaining power and can be either above or below the channel-profit-maximizing level, whereas under two-part tariffs, wholesale prices are set to maximize bilateral surplus independently of bargaining power but are systematically too low from an overall channel perspective because of rent shifting across relationships. As a result, neither contractual form uniformly dominates; instead, relative performance depends on how bargaining shapes gains from trade across firms. When bargaining leads to slotting fees, two-part tariffs yield higher channel profits than linear contracts, whereas when it would imply payments from retailers to suppliers, linear contracts may perform better. These findings offer a unified explanation for the persistence of linear contracts alongside two-part tariffs, the direction of fixed payments, and the conditions under which each contractual form is likely to arise.     We consider mixed-integer quadratic optimization problems with banded matrices and indicator variables. These problems arise pervasively in statistical inference problems with time-series data, where the banded matrix captures the temporal relationship of the underlying process. - [Optimize-via-Estimate: How to Make Finite-Sample Corrections for Sampling Uncertainty in Data-Driven Optimization](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2023.0682)

  - Authors: Gar Goei Loke; Taozeng Zhu; Ruiting Zuo

  - Crossref journal: Operations Research
  - Abstract:
    We propose a novel procedure for correcting for the effects of sampling and parameter uncertainty in data-driven convex optimization. We consider the data-driven setting wherein the distribution lies in a parametric family with unknown parameter. The decision-maker possesses a historical data set, from which solves a prescriptive solution as a function mapping such a data set to decisions, and measures its out-of-sample performance as an average over data sets of the same size under the sampling distribution. As the parameter is unknown to the decision-maker, we argue that this problem is ill-defined and further prove that there cannot exist solutions generalizably optimal over all parameters. As such, we formulate the problem as a weighted multi-criteria optimization with weights defined by a density (termed ‘prior’) over the uncertain parameter, and call its optimal solution prior optimal. We prove that sufficient conditions for prior optimality reduces to functions of the sufficient statistic of the parametric family, and this function can be solved as a point-wise optima of a corrected function of the original objective, with terms accounting for parameter uncertainty and sampling uncertainty respectively. The correction for sampling interpretation is verified by a close to (but not) zero ex-ante regret against a perfect information oracle in numerical illustrations on the newsvendor and portfolio optimization problems.

  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT This paper studies how the accuracy of a short‐term performance measure affects its value when a principal decides whether to retain or fire an agent, who can manipulate that measure. Compared with ignoring performance and never firing the agent, the performance measure is valuable when the savings in the expected cost of the retention error is more than the expected cost of the firing error. A more accurate performance measure can have a non‐monotonic effect on its value: Greater accuracy improves the principal's ability to correctly identify the agent, which increases efficient firing, but it causes the incompetent agent to increase manipulation to avoid being fired, which increases inefficient retention. With a more accurate performance measure, the manipulation effect dominates, and an increase in accuracy decreases its value. The results imply that the principal prefers an imperfect performance measure when the agent can choose the level of manipulation.
