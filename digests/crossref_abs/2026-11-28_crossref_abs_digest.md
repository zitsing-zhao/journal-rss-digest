- [Payout Taxes Matter: The Financing R&amp;D Mechanism](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.03394)
  - DOI: 10.1287/mnsc.2023.03394
  - Authors: James R. Brown; Gustav Martinsson
  - Affiliations: Adam C. Sinn ‘00 Department of Finance, Mays Business School, Texas A&M University, College Station, Texas 77843; Stockholm Business School, Stockholm University, Stockholm, SE-106 91 Sweden; and Sweden and Swedish House of Finance, SE-113 50 Stockholm, Sweden
  - Published: 2026-11-27
  - Crossref journal: Management Science
  - Abstract:
    Higher taxes on corporate payouts increase the cost of financing with stock issues. As a result, corporate investments that rely on external equity financing should be particularly sensitive to payout tax rates. A large literature shows that firms rely extensively on stock issues to fund research and development (R&D), suggesting that the financing of R&D is a potentially important microlevel mechanism linking payout taxation with real corporate activity. We document a robust negative association between dividend tax rates and firm R&D investment in a broad sample of developed countries between 2000 and 2019. The negative relation between dividend taxes and R&D is concentrated in equity-dependent firms in high-R&D industries. R&D is more sensitive to dividend tax rates in countries with stock market–based financial systems. Dividend taxes are less important for investment in physical capital than for R&D. Higher dividend taxes are associated with less corporate financing with stock issues, particularly in firms with low internal cash flow. Our study highlights the importance of focusing on equity-dependent activities and firms to evaluate the real consequences of payout taxation. This paper was accepted by Gustavo Manso, finance.   - Authors: Libing Fang; Xindan Li; Avanidhar Subrahmanyam; Ke Zhang
  - Affiliations: School of Management and Engineering, Jiangsu Key Laboratory of Digital Finance, Nanjing University, Nanjing, Jiangsu 210093, China; Anderson Graduate School of Management, University of California at Los Angeles, Los Angeles, California 90095; School of Digital Economics and Management, Nanjing University, Suzhou, Jiangsu 215163, China
  - Published: 2026-11-27
  - Crossref journal: Management Science
  - Abstract:
    We empirically examine the link between bank-specific innovations in financial technology (FinTech) and traditional banks’ fundamentals. We propose a new measure for FinTech innovation as the textual similarity of banks’ patent filings to standard FinTech policy documents. We show that FinTech innovation implies enhanced efficiency, profitability, and operating capacity (e.g., cost management, funding and lending activity). FinTech also ameliorates banks’ risk measures, including overall risk (Z-score), the liquidity, and nonperforming loan ratios. Our heterogeneity analysis suggests that banks with stronger managerial capacity and higher labor intensity derive moderately greater benefits from FinTech innovations. Further analysis reveals that FinTech-driven lending expansions focus on unsecured and low-risk loans, with technological specializations in cloud computing. We also find supportive evidence that expanding innovation in artificial intelligence and blockchain can yield performance gains to commercial banks. This paper was accepted by Kay Giesecke, finance. - [Intended and Unintended Consequences of the                     <scp>FASB</scp>                     's Revised Liability Definition: Evidence From Constructive Obligations](https://doi.org/10.1111/1911-3846.70104)
  - DOI: 10.1111/1911-3846.70104
  - Authors: Shannon M. Garavaglia; Cassie A. Mongold; Brian J. White
  - Affiliations: Accounting Department, Katz Graduate School of Business University of Pittsburgh Pittsburgh Pennsylvania USA; Department of Accountancy, Gies College of Business University of Illinois at Urbana‐Champaign Champaign Illinois USA; Samuel Curtis Johnson Graduate School of Management, Cornell SC Johnson College of Business Ithaca New York USA
  - Published: 2026-11-27
  - Crossref journal: Contemporary Accounting Research
  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT We examine consequences of the FASB's recent revision to the liability definition, which repositions “present obligation” to emphasize obligation existence over future economic outflows as the primary defining characteristic of a liability. The new definition achieves its intended effect: Individuals place greater weight on present obligations and less weight on future outflows when identifying liabilities. However, individuals are also substantially less likely to identify constructive liabilities arising from customary business practices—a result arguably inconsistent with the FASB's intent. Additional experiments establish that differential emphasis drives this effect and that individuals apply a legal standard when assessing whether an obligation exists—one that customary practice does not meet. We also find the new definition biases experienced accounting professionals toward nonrecognition and provides a basis for resisting future standards proposing the recognition of constructive liabilities. Interviews with professional investors reveal strong demand for information about constructive liabilities, suggesting a misalignment between the revised definition and users' information needs.

- [Unions and Artificial Intelligence: Negotiating Technological Change in the Norwegian Financial Sector](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjir.70088)
  - DOI: 10.1111/bjir.70088
  - Authors: Fredrik Bakkemo Kostøl; Sander I. Harefallet; Marcus B. Monsvoll
  - Affiliations: Department of Industrial Economics and Technology Management Norwegian University of Science and Technology Trondheim Norway; Norwegian University of Science and Technology Trondheim Norway
  - Published: 2026-11-27
  - Crossref journal: British Journal of Industrial Relations
  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT Artificial intelligence (AI) is spreading rapidly in the workplace, yet evidence on how unions respond to and shape its adoption remains limited. This article examines union engagement with AI in the Norwegian financial sector. Using a mixed‐methods design, we combine register analysis, a survey of union representatives and a case study of Microsoft Copilot's roll‐out in a major Nordic financial corporation. The register analysis shows a modest but robust positive association between unionisation and occupational AI exposure, indicating that unionised workers are somewhat more concentrated in occupations whose tasks are potentially affected by AI. Qualitative evidence shows that union representatives adopt a pragmatic stance: They accept AI's potential benefits but emphasise governance rules, inclusive training and the protection of employee interests. In practice, union engagement centres on conditions of use, particularly monitoring, data use and competence development, whereas core adoption decisions remain largely management‐driven. The findings highlight a central limit of institutionalised worker participation: Involvement in implementation does not necessarily extend to control over technological choices or the distribution of productivity gains.

- [Standardization participation and exploitative and exploratory innovation](https://doi.org/10.1108/ijopm-03-2026-0329)
  - DOI: 10.1108/ijopm-03-2026-0329
  - Authors: Yong Zhang; Mengli Zhao; Shibin Sheng; Jinxin Liu
  - Affiliations: School of Management, Jinan University , ,; Economics and Management School, Wuhan University , ,; Research Center for Organizational Marketing, Wuhan University , ,; Collat School of Business, University of Alabama at Birmingham , , ,; School of Management, Huazhong University of Science and Technology , ,
  - Published: 2026-12-01
  - Crossref journal: International Journal of Operations &amp; Production Management
  - Abstract:
    Purpose Firms participate in the development of technical standards to coordinate interfaces and reduce uncertainty, yet the implications of such participation for the direction of firms’ technological search remain underexplored. This study aims to examine how standardization participation shapes exploitative and exploratory innovation and how institutional conditions shape these relationships. Design/methodology/approach The study uses a firm–year panel of 486 publicly listed Chinese manufacturing firms from 2011 to 2020 to examine the relationships between standardization participation and exploitative and exploratory innovation, together with the moderating effects of institutional conditions. The complementary analysis leverages China’s 2015 standardization reform as a quasi-natural experiment. By encouraging and facilitating firms’ engagement in standardization, the reform provides a complementary setting for examining the innovation consequences of greater standardization engagement. Findings Standardization participation is positively associated with exploitative innovation but negatively associated with exploratory innovation. Legal effectiveness strengthens the positive association with exploitative innovation and the negative association with exploratory innovation. Political connections mitigate the negative association with exploratory innovation. The complementary difference-in-differences analysis shows that, following the 2015 standardization reform, firms in covered industries exhibited higher exploitative innovation and lower exploratory innovation relative to firms in non-covered industries. Originality/value This study extends research on standardization participation by showing that its innovation consequences are directionally asymmetric rather than uniformly positive. It contributes to operations management research by integrating operational coordination and technological search perspectives, showing that coordination and knowledge benefits can coexist with path-shaping consequences that favor familiar technological trajectories. A routine-based path-dependence account explains this pattern. The study further shows that legal effectiveness and political connections differentially condition these directional consequences.
