  - Published: 2026-11-10
  - Crossref journal: The Accounting Review
  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT Analyzing a cross-country sample, we provide evidence implying that private firm disclosure transparency narrows information asymmetry, which, in turn, lowers public firms' reliance on bank debt. Channel tests help substantiate that private firm disclosure elicits this positive externality, evident in lower monitoring demand, lower borrowing costs, and higher credit rating quality.   - Published: 2026-11-11
  - Crossref journal: Operations Research
  - Abstract:
    Assortment optimization has been extensively explored in recent decades due to its practical significance. Despite the substantial literature on optimization algorithms and latent score estimation, there remains a need to explore uncertainty quantification for the optimal assortment. Instead of estimating and recovering the complete optimal offer set, decision-makers may be interested in testing whether a given property holds for the optimal assortment. For instance, they may wish to determine whether certain products of interest should be included in the optimal set or ascertain how many categories of products the optimal set should contain. This paper proposes a novel inferential framework for testing such properties within the context of the widely adopted multinomial logit (MNL) model. In this model, each customer is assumed to purchase an item within the offered products with a probability proportional to the underlying preference score associated with the product. We reduce inferring a general optimal assortment property to quantifying the uncertainty associated with the sign change point detection of the marginal revenue gaps. We establish the asymptotic normality of the marginal revenue gap estimator and construct a maximum statistic using the gap estimators to detect the sign change point. By approximating the distribution of the maximum statistic through multiplier bootstrap techniques, we propose a valid testing procedure. Numerical experiments are also conducted to assess the performance of our method.

  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT Governments worldwide are concerned that multinational enterprises (MNEs) shift profits across borders to avoid taxes. While prior research shows that home‐country tax enforcement can reduce tax avoidance, far less is known about whether foreign tax authorities (FTAs) can constrain income shifting by MNE affiliates. We measure FTA monitoring using data on FTAs' downloads of US MNEs' annual reports filed with the SEC. We find that FTA monitoring is more intensive in higher‐tax‐rate countries with a larger MNE presence, consistent with those jurisdictions facing stronger incentives to monitor income shifting.   - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT Digital transformation (DT) fundamentally alters organizational structures, practices, and values, frequently generating significant friction as incoming digital artefacts structurally misalign with legacy institutional demands. These demands are rooted in institutional logics—the deeply held belief systems and practices that guide organizational behaviour. Drawing on a 15‐year longitudinal case study of a Swedish municipality, this study investigates how public‐sector entities manage this friction over time. The findings reveal DT as a dynamic, recursive trajectory driven by three sociotechnical mechanisms: Consolidation, Control, and Negotiation.     Abstract Achieving ambitious climate goals hinges on radical green innovation, yet how inventor-team composition shapes the radicalness of green patents remains largely unexplored.   - Abstract:
    Businesses use a range of strategies to manage online customer service, with social media serving as a popular platform. A growing practice in social-media customer service is the use of generic responses to address customer inquiries. Our study investigates the impact of these generic responses on customer engagement and satisfaction. We analyze social media data from one of the largest banks in the United States and apply machine-learning techniques to extract relevant information from textual data.
