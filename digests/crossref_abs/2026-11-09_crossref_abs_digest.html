4. When AI thinks about culture: comparing language-based and empirical measures of individualism–collectivism
Plamen Akaliyski; Wolfgang Messner
Journal of International Business Studies
ABS 4* | 2026-11-09 | DOI 10.1057/s41267-026-00913-3
Abstract:
Abstract Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to generate cultural insights, yet little is known about their cultural expertise and the extent to which their outputs replicate or correct for cultural stereotypes. This study compares country-level estimates of individualism–collectivism (I-C) derived from five different LLMs with a recently validated I-C index based on nationally representative surveys. The LLM-based estimates correlate highly with the survey-based index (up to r = 0.874) but also exhibit systematic deviation from the benchmark: Western societies are consistently overestimated on individualism, while Confucian East Asian societies are underestimated. Statistically, the type of benchmark-relative deviations suggests group-specific baseline distortions rather than random noise, with smaller and older models showing weaker alignment. Moreover, the deviations are systematically aligned with Hofstede’s original I-C rankings, suggesting that LLM-generated cultural representations may reflect information from legacy cultural frameworks alongside contemporary empirical patterns. These findings demonstrate both the potential and the risks of using LLMs as cultural interpreters. In consequential settings, LLM-generated cultural insights should be used responsibly—with calibration, human oversight, and awareness of potential biases. Used uncritically, they risk reinforcing outdated narratives that misdirect academic research and managerial decisions.

17. U.S. Banks’ Exposures to Climate Transition Risks
Hyeyoon Jung; Joao A. C. Santos; Lee Seltzer
Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis
ABS 4 | 2026-11-09 | DOI 10.1017/s0022109026103226
Federal Reserve Bank of New York; Federal Reserve Bank of New York and Nova School of Business and Economics
Abstract:
Abstract We propose a new approach to estimate banks’ credit exposures to transition risks that combines sectoral effects of climate policies from general equilibrium (GE) models with historical information on loans’ default risks. At worst, estimated exposures reach 14% of bank loan portfolio values. Accounting for historic loan payoff structures reduces exposures to 0.5%–2%. Exposures can increase by 3–5 percentage points due to aggregate economic shocks. Analyses surrounding climate transition events suggest our estimates can serve as an upper bound on banks’ transition risk exposures. Highlighting our measure’s novelty, emissions explain at most 60% of variation in banks’ exposures.
18. Countercyclical Risk Aversion: Evidence from 10 Million Auto Insurance Transactions in China
Huafeng (Jason) Chen; Yinglu Deng; Pengfei Li; Hulai Zhang; Hao Zhou
Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis
ABS 4 | 2026-11-09 | DOI 10.1017/s0022109026103500
Fudan University International School of Finance; Tsinghua University PBC School of Finance; Shanghai Institute of Finance for Technology Entrepreneurship and Tsinghua University PBC School of Finance; Tsinghua University PBC School of Finance and Southern University of Science and Technology College of Business
Abstract:
Abstract Whether risk aversion is time-varying and countercyclical is central to modern asset pricing, yet evidence remains limited and is based mainly on experimental, survey, or aggregate stock market data. We provide individual-level evidence from 10 million Chinese auto insurance contracts from 2011 to 2017, estimating policyholders’ risk aversion from deductible choices. We find that risk aversion is time-varying and countercyclical. The estimates are negatively related to lottery and stock trading, positively related to insurance sales and bond trading, and vary with psychological factors, including seasonal mood, “zodiac year,” and calendar events.

20. Climate change and individual behavior
René Bernard; Panagiota Tzamourani; Michael Weber
Review of Finance
ABS 4 | 2025-12-16 | DOI 10.1093/rof/rfaf053
Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy , Chausseestraße 23 , Berlin 10115,; Deutsche Bundesbank Research Centre, , Postfach 10 06 02 , Frankfurt am Main 60006,; Purdue University Daniels School of Business and NBER , 403 Mitch Daniels Blvd , West Lafayette, Indiana 47907,
Abstract:
Abstract We study the causal effect of providing information about climate change on individuals’ willingness to pay (WTP) to offset carbon emissions of flights. Receiving truthful information about ways to reduce CO2 emissions increases individuals’ WTP for CO2 offsetting. In a follow-up survey, we document individuals also increase their portfolio share in green stocks when learning about the possible benefits of investing in green firms for the environment but only if the information is not paired with the information on lower expected returns. Finally, we study the endogenous information acquisition of respondents. Individuals choose information that aligns with their views. Our results show the possibility to motivate individuals to engage in environmentally friendly behavior via information campaigns but also the challenges policymakers would need to navigate.
21. The value of guarantor monitoring: evidence from bond defaults in China
Haoyu Gao; Yuting Huang; Jingyuan Mo
Review of Finance
ABS 4 | 2025-12-08 | DOI 10.1093/rof/rfaf067
Renmin University of China School of Finance, , Beijing, 100872,; Capital University of Economics and Business International School of Economics and Management, , Beijing, 100070,; NYU Shanghai Business Department, , Shanghai, 200124,
Abstract:
Abstract Using a bond–year panel dataset, we show that external credit guarantors can mitigate default risk in the corporate bond market, and the observed effects cannot be fully attributed to pre-issuance screening. Consistent with a monitoring channel, we explore the post-issuance dynamics and find that the risk-reduction effect of guarantees is significantly stronger when guarantors face greater risk exposure or when issuers experience post-issuance financial deterioration. We further demonstrate that guarantees act as substitutes for covenant-based monitoring and promote disciplined firm behavior: Guaranteed firms exhibit reduced risk-taking, reflected in lower levels of overinvestment and excessive leverage. Together, these findings underscore the delegated monitoring role of guarantors in mitigating agency conflicts between issuers and investors in public debt markets.
22. Governance of Hybrid Fintech Ecosystem: An Examination Using the Generativity‐Control Paradigm
Deepak Chandrashekar; Krishna Satyanarayana; Anuradha Sharma; R. Srinivasan
Information Systems Journal
ABS 4 | 2026-11-08 | DOI 10.1111/isj.70068
Indian Institute of Management Bangalore Bengaluru India
Abstract:
ABSTRACT Digital platforms are increasingly leveraged to address social challenges in the financial sector. However, the fintech sector, particularly in developing economies, is inherently hybrid, creating distinct governance challenges. This study examined the applicability of generativity‐control governance literature to managing dual motives, a federated structure, and a trust‐based value orientation in hybrid fintech ecosystems. Drawing on the digital platform ecosystem governance literature as the theoretical foundation and the concept of holons from information ecology theory as the analytical lens, the study analysed interview data from 41 respondents representing the regulator, ecosystem orchestrator, fintech startups, financial institutions, incubators, and media in the Indian fintech ecosystem. It employed deductive reflexive thematic analysis to analyse the interview data and used secondary data to corroborate the findings. Accordingly, this study conceptualised complementors as holons exhibiting part‐whole behaviour, offering a novel theoretical explanation of how they maintain the generativity‐control balance and address governance challenges to achieve both economic and social outcomes simultaneously in hybrid fintech ecosystems. It also identified and defined three complementary governance mechanisms (demarcation, regulation, and legitimization) that address governance challenges associated with the ecosystem's motives, structure, and value orientation, respectively. Ultimately, the findings extend the information systems governance literature and contribute to the fintech literature by explaining how complementors' collaborative participation, their use of digital public infrastructure, and the provision of regulatory sandboxes within the Indian fintech ecosystem facilitate the generativity‐control balance.

26. Unpacking the multidimensionality of gender role attitudes: The interactive role of territory and social class in Italy
Lorenzo Cattani; Giovanni Amerigo Giuliani; Nicola De Luigi
Sociology
ABS 4 | 2026-11-09 | DOI 10.1177/00380385261468599
Department of the Arts, University of Bologna, Italy; Department of Political and Social Sciences, University of Bologna, Italy
Abstract:
This study examines how occupational class and territorial context interact to shape gender role attitudes in Italy. Using 2017 European Values Study data ( N = 1757), we analyze attitudes toward women’s labor market participation and caregiving responsibilities. Results reveal distinct patterns: territorial context primarily drives public sphere attitudes, with northern regions significantly more egalitarian than the South. Conversely, occupational class predominantly shapes private sphere attitudes, with post-industrial classes supporting more egalitarian domestic arrangements than blue-collar workers. These findings indicate the relative influence of class versus territory depends on the attitudinal domain examined, challenging uniform applications of modernization theory and highlighting how material conditions and occupational socialization jointly condition gender attitude formation in advanced economies with uneven development.
