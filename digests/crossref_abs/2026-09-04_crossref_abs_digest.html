Traders devote significant resources to producing private information. The value of such information is eroded when it is leaked. We study the use of multiple brokers by institutions to help mitigate information leakage. We document that trades using multiple brokers better predict future returns and have higher future profits, consistent with information possessed by investors driving the decision to use multiple brokers. We examine the value of skilled immigrants in finance by exploiting evidence from visa lotteries. We find that hedge fund management companies that secure more H-1B visas in random lotteries deliver higher alphas, Sharpe ratios, and information ratios. The superior performance of funds with high H-1B visa allocations can be attributed to well-paid and highly educated H-1B workers with quantitative skills. H-1B workers add value by helping hedge funds develop distinctive investment strategies, arbitrage prominent stock anomalies, and overcome capacity constraints.
