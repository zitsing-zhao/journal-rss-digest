  - Published: 2026-10-12
  - Crossref journal: The Accounting Review
  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT Audit teams may consult with their firm’s national office to obtain specialized expertise, satisfy regulatory and quality control requirements, and demonstrate due professional care. We describe the consultation process based on PCAOB standards, firm methodologies, and auditor interviews. We explain different types of consultations and highlight differences in the determinants and usefulness of mandatory versus voluntary consultations. Using PCAOB inspection data, we show that formal consultations are largely predictable based on engagement characteristics and, on average, are not associated with audit quality. Collectively, our findings deepen our understanding of auditors’ use of national office consultations and their implications for audit practice.     When Sharing the Same Information Is Optimal Should a school advisor tailor recommendation letters to different employers or share the same information with everyone? Similar questions arise in start-up fundraising and biotech partnerships. In “Optimality of Public Persuasion for Single-Good Allocation,” Chen and Qi study how a sender can strategically disclose information when allocating an indivisible good to at most one self-interested receiver. The sender may send tailored private messages to individual receivers or publicly broadcast the same information to everyone. Surprisingly, the authors show that, when the sender has a known preference ordering over receivers, public persuasion is optimal even if receivers can subsequently communicate and share information.
