Using a panel of U.S. corporate tax return data from 2004 to 2018, we show that IPO completion is associated with the rapid implementation of multinational foreign tax structures.     This article provides a specification test for semiparametric models with nonparametrically generated regressors. Such regressors are not observed by the researcher but are nonparametrically identified and estimable.     Abstract While existing scientometric research has examined how individual network position and team composition affect citation-based performance, less is known about how the structural configuration of socio-semantic networks relates to collective publication output. We propose that publication output is associated with configurations that foster mutual visibility between research topics (memes) and author communities. Drawing on mutualistic network theory from ecology, we hypothesise that lower bipartite modularity (H1) and higher nestedness (H2) are associated with higher publication output. We analyse 6,406 publications across three ecosystem approaches to health (EcoHealth, One Health, Planetary Health) over 18 approach × period observations (2004–2023), linking author communities to 20 research topics identified by Latent Dirichlet Allocation. Given the observational design, we report these as field-level patterns, not causal mechanisms. The non-significant association for H2, combined with marked algorithmic variability, invites reconsidering how nestedness is operationalised in socio-semantic contexts. By integrating social and semantic dimensions within a mutualistic framework, this approach complements existing scientometric tools.
