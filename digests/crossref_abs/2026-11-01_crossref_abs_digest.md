- [The Effect of Reducing Welfare Access on Employment, Health, and Children’s Long-Run Outcomes](https://doi.org/10.1093/ej/ueag141)
  - DOI: 10.1093/ej/ueag141
  - Authors: Jeffrey Hicks; Gaëlle Simard-Duplain; David A Green; William Warburton
  - Affiliations: University of Toronto; Carleton University; University of British Columbia; Enterprise Economic Consulting
  - Published: 2026-10-31
  - Crossref journal: The Economic Journal
  - Abstract:
    Abstract Welfare caseloads in North America halved following reforms in the 1990s and 2000s. We study how this shift affected families by linking Canadian welfare records to tax returns, medical care, educational attainment, and criminal charges. We find substantial and heterogeneous employment responses that increased average income despite reduced transfers. We find zero effects on aggregate healthcare costs, but mothers saw reduced preventative care and increased mental health treatment, consistent with the transition to employment elevating time pressure and stress. We find no effect on teenagers’ education or criminal charges as young adults but find some evidence of intergenerational welfare transmission.
