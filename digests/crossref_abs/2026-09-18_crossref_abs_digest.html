Prophet Inequalities for a New Class of Overbooking Problems in Container Shipping
2026-09-17 | DOI: 10.1287/opre.2024.0842 | Operations Research
Authors: Jacob Feldman; Yukai Huang; Panos Kouvelis
Affiliations: Olin Business School, Washington University, Saint Louis, Missouri 63130; Sawyer Business School, Suffolk University, Boston, Massachusetts 02108
A New Class of Overbooking Problems in Container Shipping Industry Container carriers lose billions of dollars each year when shippers reserve slots but fail to show up with the corresponding cargo. This paper studies an online booking problem under a deposit-based mechanism designed to better align incentives and mitigate the costs of overbooking. The authors derive near-optimal online policies for two demand settings: one in which shippers’ no-show behavior is coupled through a spot market, and another in which no-shows are independent. In the coupled setting, a randomized threshold policy guarantees at least 81.9% of clairvoyant profit. In the independent setting, a simple greedy policy is shown to be asymptotically 0.5-optimal as the number of slots tends to infinity. Extensive simulations further demonstrate strong and robust practical performance.

Authors: Salma Mousa; Lennard Naumann; Alexandra Scacco
Affiliations: University of California, Los Angeles; WZB Berlin Social Science Center; Humboldt University of Berlin; University of Hamburg
Abstract Intergroup contact interventions often yield limited results in deeply divided societies. We test contact and empathy education in Lebanon, where Syrian refugees comprise a quarter of the population. In a fully cross-randomised field experiment with 887 Lebanese and Syrian youth embedded in a psychosocial support programme, we find that contact has little effect on outgroup attitudes and behaviour, while empathy education consistently improves attitudes for Lebanese youth and behavioural engagement for both. The combined treatment achieves little beyond empathy alone, with no evidence of spillovers to parents (n = 595). In polarised settings, empathy education should precede — or replace — contact.

ABSTRACT Salary History Bans (SHBs) prohibit employers from requesting applicants' salary histories, shifting bargaining power toward workers and reducing employers' information about worker productivity. We find that SHB adoption is associated with lower product differentiation, particularly among firms with greater exposure to labor costs and innovative investment, greater reliance on skilled labor and exposure to hiring frictions, and rents not already contested through collective bargaining. Transaction Costs and the Gains from Trade in Water Markets
2026-09-08 | DOI: 10.1162/rest.a.1861 | Review of Economics and Statistics
Authors: Nick Hagerty
Affiliations: Montana State University nicholas.hagerty@montana.edu
Abstract Surface water in California is scarce and tightly managed, yet little is traded through a market. This paper studies transaction costs in the existing wholesale water market and estimates the benefits of reducing them. I develop an empirical framework that uses transactions among water districts to analyze welfare, decomposing prices into demand and transaction costs and simulating a market with only physical frictions. Transaction costs are large: median variable costs are 54% of the price, and fixed costs deter many districts from participating. Eliminating them produces gains of several hundred million dollars per year, or 1-3% of statewide water expenditures.

Abstract Many governments use fiscal transfers to reduce regional inequality, yet discretionary allocation may also depend on informal communication among officials. We examine this mechanism in a large central-to-provincial transfer system, using policymakers’ shared workplace, alumni, and hometown backgrounds to measure social ties. Exploiting ministerial turnover and controlling for provincial leadership, we find that prior workplace ties significantly increase transfers. We then embed these estimates in a quantitative spatial model with trade, migration, input-output linkages, and fiscal-productivity effects. Abstract Debates over return-to-office mandates have intensified since the COVID-19 pandemic ended, though their economic implications remain poorly understood. Using large-scale resume data, we analyze how these policies affect employee tenure and seniority at three large U.S. tech companies: Microsoft, SpaceX, and Apple. Employing a distributional synthetic controls framework, we estimate a reduction in tenure and seniority at firms returning to the office. Abstract What are the consequences of losing political representation in a democracy? Abstract We test whether loan officers discriminate against female business owners in capital decisions affecting real businesses in an Ethiopian business plan competition. Abstract We quantify the benefits of better firm-to-firm matching in an aggregate diffusion model where individuals acquire profitable knowledge from others. We estimate the model using a small-scale program in Kenya that creates new opportunities for firm managers to interact. ABSTRACT This paper contends that the concept of risk is fundamentally shaped by two distinct motivating ideas: (1) that something undesirable may occur (dominant in safety, security, and engineering contexts), and (2) that something unexpected may occur (central to many business, strategic, and decision‐making domains).
