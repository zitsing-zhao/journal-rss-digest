Authors: Sharon Mavin; Nic Beech; Paul Hibbert
Affiliations: Newcastle University Newcastle upon Tyne NE1 7RU UK; University of Salford Salford M5 4WT UK; The University of St Andrews St Andrews KY16 9AJ UK
Abstract We approach this essay as individuals who have (sometimes painful) experience of vulnerable leadership and a desire to learn from this experience. We root our work in debates about dialogue and its relationship with reflexivity. We establish, through building on the relevant literature, how shared dialogue and reflexivity can support learning from vulnerable leadership experiences – and how these learning experiences can involve moral emotions, particularly shame and guilt. We use vignettes constructed through our own reflexive dialogue as illustrations to show how the process of learning from reflexive dialogue can play out. Overall, our essay outlines a pedagogy and principles for vulnerable leadership learning, both in the context of formal programmes and in ongoing practice.

This article tells the story of a worker-led initiative to form a Works Council at a Q-commerce platform in Germany. From the viewpoint of a rider involved, the article first describes the widespread dissatisfaction of workers with management, which created the need for organising. The article then narrates the difficulties in ‘recruiting’ engaged colleagues to the Works Council initiative among a transient workforce, the eventual success of the ‘workers’ assembly’ and, finally, the shock of the ‘Hub’s’ entire workforce being fired in an act of platform capitalist ‘union busting’.
