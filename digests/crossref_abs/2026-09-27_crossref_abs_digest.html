Authors: Jesper Akesson; Rajat Kochhar; Robert W Hahn; Robert D Metcalfe
Affiliations: The Behaviouralist; University of Chicago; Carnegie Mellon University & Technology Policy Institute; Columbia University & NBER
Abstract Water suppliers are showing greater interest in using residential water audits to promote conservation. Yet there is limited causal evidence on their effectiveness, persistence, and welfare implications. We address this gap using a natural field experiment in the United Kingdom that randomly assigns about 45,000 households to receive different behavioural encouragements to complete an online home water audit. Our analysis yields three main findings. First, financial incentives substantially increase audit take-up and lead to large short-run reductions in water use: among compliers, consumption falls by about 17 per cent over a two-month period. 2026-09-26 | DOI: 10.1177/09500170261473534 | Work, Employment and Society
Authors: Chiara Benassi; Lisa Dorigatti
Affiliations: University of Bologna, Italy; University of Milan, Italy
Drawing on 68 semi-structured interviews with assembly-line workers and other informants, this article examines workers’ non-compliant behaviour in an Italian manufacturing company. Our research underscores that workers engage in non-compliance not to defy managerial authority, but to respond to management-created inefficiencies stemming from excessive standardization and cost-cutting. We argue that this behaviour cannot be adequately accounted for through the dominant conceptions of non-compliant behaviour at work such as organizational misbehaviour and games, and can be better understood via the concept of ‘workaround’ developed in the information systems literature.
