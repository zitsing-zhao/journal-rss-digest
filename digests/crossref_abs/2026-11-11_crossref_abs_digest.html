1. Private Firm Disclosures and Public Firms' Debt Choice
Fengqin Chen; Jeffrey Pittman; Walid Saffar
Accounting Review
ABS 4* | 2026-11-10 | DOI 10.2308/tar-2024-0646
Shanghai University of Finance and Economics; Memorial University; Virginia Tech; The Hong Kong Polytechnic University
Abstract:
ABSTRACT Analyzing a cross-country sample, we provide evidence implying that private firm disclosure transparency narrows information asymmetry, which, in turn, lowers public firms' reliance on bank debt. Channel tests help substantiate that private firm disclosure elicits this positive externality, evident in lower monitoring demand, lower borrowing costs, and higher credit rating quality. Cross-sectional results suggest that the impact of private firm transparency is larger when public firms suffer worse information opacity, private firm disclosures are more important to their public peers, and bond markets are more developed. In relying on the staggered implementation of electronic business registers as a plausibly exogenous shock and a simulated disclosure scope as the instrumental variable, we continue to find that private firm disclosures reduce public firms' bank debt reliance, helping to alleviate endogeneity threats. Data Availability Statement: The data used in this study are obtained from publicly available sources identified in the paper. JEL Classifications: G32; G21; M41.

4. Uncertainty Attitudes, Subjective Beliefs, and Decisions Under Uncertainty
Adeline Delavande; Jayant Ganguli; Friederike Mengel
Management Science
ABS 4* | 2026-11-10 | DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2024.06232
Department of Economics, University of Technology Sydney, Ultimo, New South Wales 2007, Australia; Department of Economics, University of Essex, Colchester CO4 3SQ, United Kingdom; Department of Economics, University of Essex, Colchester CO4 3SQ, United Kingdom; and Department of Applied Economics, Erasmus University Rotterdam, Burgemeester Oudlaan 50, Rotterdam 3062 PA, Netherlands
Abstract:
We conducted survey experiments to gather new data on uncertainty attitudes and subjective beliefs. Our survey targets a general population, focusing on health and financial decisions. We collect data on (i) individual attitudes toward risk and ambiguity, including individual-specific probability weighting and loss aversion, and (ii) individual probabilistic beliefs about events related to the decisions we consider. The joint availability of individual preference parameters and subjective beliefs allows us to study heterogeneity and cross-correlations across these parameters in depth and to estimate structural models of decision making under uncertainty that go beyond subjective expected utility. We find that both preferences and beliefs are highly heterogeneous. Compared with subjective expected utility, prospect theory combined with source-dependent probability weighting fits the data strictly better in some contexts and equally well in others. Prospect theory performs particularly well when the belief distribution is concentrated toward extreme values. 5. Recommender Systems Under Privacy Protection
Can Küçükgül; Özalp Özer; Shouqiang Wang
Management Science
ABS 4* | 2026-11-10 | DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2024.05007
Rutgers School of Business, Camden, New Jersey 08102; FBA Science, Selling Partner Services, Amazon, Seattle, Washington 98109; and Naveen Jindal School of Management, The University of Texas at Dallas, Richardson, Texas 75080; Naveen Jindal School of Management, The University of Texas at Dallas, Richardson, Texas 75080
Abstract:
We examine how a profit-driven platform designs its recommender policies under varying privacy protection regimes. We demonstrate a functional equivalence between opt-out protection and unprotected privacy. Notably, granting consumers the autonomy to misrepresent personal data can compel the platform to distort recommendations, inadvertently harming both parties. When recommendations are purely informative, such privacy controls can deter personalization entirely. However, when recommendations also serve an allocative role—controlling product access—algorithmic discrimination may emerge, restricting the disadvantaged minority from valuable opportunities. These findings remain robust even with endogenous profit margins. This paper was accepted by Omar Besbes, revenue management and market analytics. 6. Deadly Disease, Fear, and Ostracism in the Credit Market
Sumit Agarwal; Pengsheng Lin; Yupeng Lin; Ruichang Lu; Xiaojun Zhang
Management Science
ABS 4* | 2026-11-10 | DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2025.01919
NUS Business School, National University of Singapore, Singapore; School of Economics and Management, Tsinghua University, Beijing 100084, China; Guanghua School of Management, Peking University, Beijing 100871, China
Abstract:
This paper exploits the 2014 U.S. Ebola outbreak as a natural experiment to examine how fear distorts mortgage credit allocation. We find a 3.8-percentage-point increase in mortgage denial rates for Black applicants during the outbreak, despite negligible infection risk. Approved Black borrowers have stronger credit profiles and lower default rates, yet are less likely to receive loan modifications when delinquent. These effects are absent in algorithm-driven FinTech lending and reverse quickly postoutbreak—patterns inconsistent with statistical discrimination. Robustness tests rule out alternative explanations related to political cycles, labor market impacts, applicant pool composition changes, and bank financial conditions. Consistent with diagnostic expectation theory, higher exposure to Ebola fear does not result in a significant learning effect during the COVID-19 pandemic. 7. Combinatorial Inference on the Optimal Assortment in Multinomial Logit Models
Shuting Shen; Xi Chen; Ethan Xingyuan Fang; Junwei Lu
Operations Research
ABS 4* | 2026-11-11 | DOI 10.1287/opre.2024.0747
Harvard University, Cambridge, Massachusetts, United States; New York University, Stern School of Business, New York, New York, United States; Duke University, Durham, North Carolina, United States
Abstract:
Assortment optimization has been extensively explored in recent decades due to its practical significance. Despite the substantial literature on optimization algorithms and latent score estimation, there remains a need to explore uncertainty quantification for the optimal assortment. Instead of estimating and recovering the complete optimal offer set, decision-makers may be interested in testing whether a given property holds for the optimal assortment. For instance, they may wish to determine whether certain products of interest should be included in the optimal set or ascertain how many categories of products the optimal set should contain. This paper proposes a novel inferential framework for testing such properties within the context of the widely adopted multinomial logit (MNL) model. In this model, each customer is assumed to purchase an item within the offered products with a probability proportional to the underlying preference score associated with the product. We reduce inferring a general optimal assortment property to quantifying the uncertainty associated with the sign change point detection of the marginal revenue gaps. We establish the asymptotic normality of the marginal revenue gap estimator and construct a maximum statistic using the gap estimators to detect the sign change point. By approximating the distribution of the maximum statistic through multiplier bootstrap techniques, we propose a valid testing procedure. Numerical experiments are also conducted to assess the performance of our method.
8. Bellman Optimality of Average-Reward Robust Markov Decision Processes with a Constant Gain
Shengbo Wang; Nian Si
Operations Research
ABS 4* | 2026-11-11 | DOI 10.1287/opre.2025.2498
University of Southern California, Daniel J. Epstein Department of Industrial and Systems Engineering, Los Angeles, California, United States; HKUST, IEDA, Hong Kong, Hong Kong
Abstract:
Learning and optimal control under robust Markov decision processes (MDPs) have received increasing attention, yet most existing theory, algorithms, and applications focus on finite-horizon or discounted models. Long-run average-reward formulations, while natural in many operations research and management contexts, remain underexplored. This is primarily because the dynamic programming foundations are technically challenging and only partially understood, with several fundamental questions remaining open. This paper steps toward a general framework for average-reward robust MDPs by analyzing the constant-gain setting. We study the average-reward robust control problem with possible information asymmetries between the controller and an S-rectangular adversary. Our analysis centers on the constant-gain robust Bellman equation, examining both the existence of solutions and their relationship to the optimal average reward. Specifically, we identify when solutions to the robust Bellman equation characterize the optimal average reward and stationary policies, and we provide one-sided weak communication conditions ensuring solutions' existence. These findings expand the dynamic programming theory for average-reward robust MDPs and lay a foundation for robust dynamic decision making under long-run average criteria in operational environments.

10. The Effect of <scp>Non‐US</scp> Tax Authority Monitoring on Income Shifting by Affiliates of US Multinationals: Evidence From <scp>EDGAR</scp> Search Activity
Sabrina Chi; Anh V. Persson; Terry Shevlin; Oktay Urcan
Contemporary Accounting Research
ABS 4 | 2026-11-10 | DOI 10.1111/1911-3846.70101
California State University, Fullerton Fullerton California USA; University of Illinois at Urbana‐Champaign Champaign Illinois USA; University of California, Irvine Irvine California USA; University of Washington Seattle Washington USA
Abstract:
ABSTRACT Governments worldwide are concerned that multinational enterprises (MNEs) shift profits across borders to avoid taxes. While prior research shows that home‐country tax enforcement can reduce tax avoidance, far less is known about whether foreign tax authorities (FTAs) can constrain income shifting by MNE affiliates. We measure FTA monitoring using data on FTAs' downloads of US MNEs' annual reports filed with the SEC. We find that FTA monitoring is more intensive in higher‐tax‐rate countries with a larger MNE presence, consistent with those jurisdictions facing stronger incentives to monitor income shifting. More importantly, we find that monitoring by an affiliate's local tax authority does not systematically reduce income shifting by US MNE affiliates, whereas aggregate monitoring in the MNE group affects an affiliate even when its own local FTA is not observably monitoring it. This result is consistent with a spillover effect of FTA monitoring. This association is stronger when FTAs operate under information‐exchange treaties, when company disclosures are more detailed, and when enforcement capacity is greater. We also find positive associations between aggregate FTA monitoring and subsequent tax avoidance and tax settlements of US MNEs. Our findings suggest monitoring by multiple FTAs effectively combats MNE tax avoidance and have implications for how tax administrations design and coordinate monitoring efforts.
11. Gender Gaps in <scp>AI</scp> Skill Acquisition and Labor Market Returns
Ting Ren; Xuhang Shen
Industrial Relations
ABS 4 | 2026-11-10 | DOI 10.1111/irel.70053
HSBC Business School Peking University Shenzhen China
Abstract:
ABSTRACT Using resumes from a major Chinese online recruitment platform, we document gender gaps in reported AI skills and their labor‐market associations. Women are less likely to report AI skills. Gelbach decompositions show that major choice, occupational sorting, and complementary skills account for part of this gap, while a sizable residual remains. Reported AI skills are associated with higher wages, but the association is weaker for women. Women with AI skills also sort less into AI‐requiring jobs. Employer responses improve when applicants' reported AI skills match job requirements, with no statistically significant gender difference detected.
12. Differences in gender pension gaps in public and private pensions in West Germany: An analysis sensitive to interdependencies across the life course
Carla Rowold
Work, Employment and Society
ABS 4 | 2026-11-10 | DOI 10.1177/09500170261482093
University of Oxford, UK; Max Planck Institute for Demographic Research, Germany; Max Planck–University of Helsinki Center for Social Inequalities in Population Health, Rostock, Germany and Helsinki, Finland; Hertie School, Germany; Einstein Center Population Diversity (ECPD), Germany
Abstract:
Gender pension gaps (GPG) surpass gender wage gaps in most European countries. Research has largely relied on single-factor, employment-centred explanations, and we know less about how GPGs emerge from an interdependent life-course perspective. This study contributes to research on gender pension gaps and cumulative (dis)advantage across the life course by applying a life-course-sensitive decomposition to linked survey–register data for Germany (SHARE-RV), comparing gender gaps in public and private pensions based on common work–family life courses, including relative earning positions, relevant due to pension privatisation in Europe. It considers the interdependencies of employment, family life and earnings over the life course. GPGs occur because privileged life courses yielding high pensions are almost exclusively accessible to fathers, while female-typical life-course patterns characterised by care work and part-time employment yield low pensions. Gendered access to high-income careers drives the GPG in private pensions more strongly than in public pensions, underscoring the future risk of high GPGs given persistent gender wage inequalities and pension privatisation in Germany.
13. Navigating Institutional Friction in Digital Transformation: A Mechanism‐Based View
Hugo Hedlund; Katrin Jonsson; Johan Sandberg
Information Systems Journal
ABS 4 | 2026-11-11 | DOI 10.1111/isj.70070
Department of Informatics Umeå University Umeå Sweden
Abstract:
ABSTRACT Digital transformation (DT) fundamentally alters organizational structures, practices, and values, frequently generating significant friction as incoming digital artefacts structurally misalign with legacy institutional demands. These demands are rooted in institutional logics—the deeply held belief systems and practices that guide organizational behaviour. Drawing on a 15‐year longitudinal case study of a Swedish municipality, this study investigates how public‐sector entities manage this friction over time. The findings reveal DT as a dynamic, recursive trajectory driven by three sociotechnical mechanisms: Consolidation, Control, and Negotiation. These mechanisms generate non‐linear shifts across institutional states of conflict and alignment. This study makes three contributions. First, it reconceptualizes DT theory by framing transformation as recursive, non‐linear cycles of divergence and convergence that redirect, suppress, or accommodate localized, logic‐based resistance. Second, it advances the logic multiplicity framework by demonstrating how digital artefacts alter sociotechnical coupling, thereby shifting the centrality and compatibility of competing demands. Third, it illuminates the causal architecture of macro‐level institutional change, detailing the observable actions used to navigate logic‐based resistance during DT.

15. Team diversity and radical green innovation: a patent-based analysis of gender, geographic, and institutional effects
Daniel Coronado; Esther Ferrándiz; Rosario Marín; Carmen Puentes
Scientometrics
ABS 4 | 2026-11-10 | DOI 10.1007/s11192-026-05867-9
Abstract:
Abstract Achieving ambitious climate goals hinges on radical green innovation, yet how inventor-team composition shapes the radicalness of green patents remains largely unexplored. Drawing on theories of knowledge heterogeneity and recombination, we jointly assess three dimensions of diversity that prior work has typically examined in isolation: gender and geographic diversity among inventors, and institutional diversity among co-assignees. We analyze 124,114 green patent families filed at the European Patent Office between 1990 and 2020 using fractional response models. Gender diversity shows a negative association with radicalness, contrasting with evidence from general, technology-wide patenting, where gender diversity has been linked to greater novelty. Geographic diversity is positively related to radicalness in the full population and remains positive among co-assigned patents, though with weaker statistical support in this smaller subsample. Institutional diversity displays a positive but modest association within the 12,267 co-assigned families for which it can be measured. Among collaboration types, partnerships with universities show a positive link, whereas inter-firm ones show a negative one. These patterns hold broadly across alternative diversity measures, estimation methods, and additional sensitivity analyses. Overall, our results demonstrate that diversity is not uniformly related to radical green innovation: its implications vary across dimensions of diversity and depend on the organizational context in which heterogeneous knowledge is integrated. These findings underscore the importance of adopting a multidimensional perspective on team diversity and offer implications for innovation management and public policies aimed at fostering radical green innovation.

17. EXPRESS: The Implications of Generic Responses in Online Customer Service Operations
Jitsama Tanlamai; Wreetabrata Kar; Warut Khern-am-nuai; Yossiri Adulyasak
Production and Operations Management
ABS 4 | 2026-11-11 | DOI 10.1177/10591478261508621
Abstract:
Businesses use a range of strategies to manage online customer service, with social media serving as a popular platform. A growing practice in social-media customer service is the use of generic responses to address customer inquiries. Our study investigates the impact of these generic responses on customer engagement and satisfaction. We analyze social media data from one of the largest banks in the United States and apply machine-learning techniques to extract relevant information from textual data. We leverage a quasi-experimental setting in which a generic response to a customer’s inquiry serves as the treatment. We find that generic responses increase the likelihood that customers reply to the bank. Among customers who reply, generic responses are associated with lower satisfaction. However, our further analyses show that under certain conditions, strategic use of generic responses is associated with higher customer satisfaction. Our findings provide guidance to operation managers regarding how generic responses can be tailored and used to improve online customer service operations.
