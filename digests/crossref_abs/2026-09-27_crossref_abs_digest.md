- [Busing to Opportunity? The Impacts of the METCO Voluntary School Desegregation Program on Urban Students of Color](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjag043)
  - DOI: 10.1093/qje/qjag043
  - Authors: Elizabeth Setren
  - Affiliations: Tufts University , Department of Economics, Medford, MA 02155
  - Published: 2026-09-26
  - Crossref journal: The Quarterly Journal of Economics
  - Abstract:
    Abstract School assignment policies are a key lever for expanding access to high-performing schools and promoting racial and socioeconomic integration. For over 50 years, the Metropolitan Council for Educational Opportunity (METCO) has bussed students of color from Boston, Massachusetts to relatively wealthier and predominantly White suburbs. I estimate the effects of attending a high-performing suburban school by comparing applicants who receive enrollment offers with applicants who do not. A two-stage least squares approach exploits waitlist assignment priorities and controls for a rich set of characteristics from birth records and application data. Attending a suburban school boosts math and English test scores, reduces dropout, and increases on-time high school graduation. Four-year college aspirations, enrollment, and graduation increase along with earnings in adulthood. Students whose parents did not attend college and boys experience the largest effects. The evidence points to several mechanisms, including access to higher value-added schools, higher achieving peers, and schools with stronger college-going norms. Participants are more likely to be tracked into lower performing classes, implying that effects could be larger with access to more advanced coursework. District value-added estimates suggest that students’ educational trajectories shift at least as much as they would from growing up in the suburban districts.

  - Published: 2026-09-26
  - Crossref journal: The Economic Journal
  - Abstract:
    Abstract Water suppliers are showing greater interest in using residential water audits to promote conservation. Yet there is limited causal evidence on their effectiveness, persistence, and welfare implications. We address this gap using a natural field experiment in the United Kingdom that randomly assigns about 45,000 households to receive different behavioural encouragements to complete an online home water audit. Our analysis yields three main findings. First, financial incentives substantially increase audit take-up and lead to large short-run reductions in water use: among compliers, consumption falls by about 17 per cent over a two-month period. The size of the incentive matters for participation but not for the magnitude of conservation. Second, conservation effects attenuate rapidly, becoming undetectable after roughly two months. As a result, the intervention yields small welfare gains, with per capita net benefits typically below Â£1 under a wide range of assumptions. Third, targeting high-use households roughly doubles short-run conservation effects but remains insufficient to generate sizable welfare gains because conservation does not persist. Overall, while audits and incentives induce meaningful short-run conservation, their policy value depends critically on whether these effects persist.

  - Published: 2026-09-26
  - Crossref journal: Work, Employment and Society
  - Abstract:
    Drawing on interviews from two case studies in China’s food delivery and house cleaning sectors, this research examines workers’ gender subjectivities and their interaction with the labour politics of platform work. Male delivery riders experience a crisis of masculinity, de-flexibilise their labour to maximise income and affirm migrant masculinities, yet their insecurity persists. Female cleaners seek to construct respectable femininities in stigmatised domestic work, juggling care and paid work across life stages and strengthening relational labour to counter algorithmic constraints. The study conceptualises platforms as gendered organisations of flexibility and algorithmic management and examines their interaction with workers’ gendered subjectivities. This interaction produces distinct gendered consequences: exposing men to intensified work and risk of injury, and women to persistent precarity and low pay. The article argues that gender subjectivities both sustain platform domination and provide limited bases for everyday resistance.

- [Non-compliance at work: Workarounds and inefficiencies in the ‘smart’ factory](https://doi.org/10.1177/09500170261473534)
  - DOI: 10.1177/09500170261473534
  - Authors: Chiara Benassi; Lisa Dorigatti
  - Affiliations: University of Bologna, Italy; University of Milan, Italy
  - Published: 2026-09-26
  - Crossref journal: Work, Employment and Society
  - Abstract:
    Drawing on 68 semi-structured interviews with assembly-line workers and other informants, this article examines workers’ non-compliant behaviour in an Italian manufacturing company. Our research underscores that workers engage in non-compliance not to defy managerial authority, but to respond to management-created inefficiencies stemming from excessive standardization and cost-cutting. We argue that this behaviour cannot be adequately accounted for through the dominant conceptions of non-compliant behaviour at work such as organizational misbehaviour and games, and can be better understood via the concept of ‘workaround’ developed in the information systems literature. By combining insights from industrial sociology, labour process theory and information systems literature, this article makes an original contribution to the sociological understanding of workers’ non-compliant behaviour and its connection to organizational inefficiencies. Our findings also shed light on the enduring importance of workers’ creative input even in highly automatable jobs, challenging techno-centric narratives of the future of work.
