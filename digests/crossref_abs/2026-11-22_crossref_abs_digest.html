3. Interrogating youth drinking declines: Youth transitions, delayed onset and the changing frequency of drinking amongst young people in the United Kingdom
Emily Nicholls; Muhammad Waqas
Sociology
ABS 4 | 2026-11-21 | DOI 10.1177/00380385261489421
University of York, UK; University of Bradford, UK
Abstract:
As transitions to adulthood become increasingly delayed and precarious across high-income countries, youth drinking has notably declined. This paper interrogates the ‘delayed onset’ theory, investigating whether – despite the ‘decline’ – individual consumption increases as young people age (meaning their alcohol consumption may eventually ‘catch up’ with that of previous cohorts). Analysing UK Household Longitudinal Study data over a decade, we examine trends in consumption frequency and Heavy Episodic Drinking. Findings demonstrate that both metrics increase as individuals age, with higher consumption associated with male gender and increased education / household income. Findings are considered through a sociological lens attendant to the precarity, unevenness and protractedness of youth transitions and the entanglements of gender, class and drinking, building on cross-sectional studies to help develop an evidence base around ‘delayed onset’. This has implications for how drinking declines are conceptualised and for broader sociological understandings of contemporary youth transitions to adulthood.
