- [The effect of the mandatory disclosure of corporate tax returns on reporting bias](https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679x.70087)
  - DOI: 10.1111/1475-679x.70087
  - Authors: ERIC J. ALLEN; HENRY L. FRIEDMAN; AYDIN UYSAL
  - Affiliations: UC Riverside; UCLA Anderson School of Management; Former Director of Equity Research at the Charles Schwab Corp. Deceased.
  - Published: 2026-10-11
  - Crossref journal: Journal of Accounting Research
  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT How do investor reactions to the mandatory disclosure of firm tax information influence managers' reporting choices? To answer this question, we build a structural model of reporting bias, which we test with a unique data set of Turkish firms that have been mandated to publicly disclose their tax returns since 2009. We find that both book and tax income numbers are informative to investors. Structural estimates are consistent with disclosure of the tax report reducing both market incentives to manage book earnings and investor uncertainty about underlying firm value. Tax disclosure is associated with higher reported tax income, though the increase is not statistically significant. Counterfactual estimates suggest that book‐tax‐conformity could change financial and tax reporting biases, but lead to a lower quality information environment relative to the separate disclosure of book and tax reports.

- [China's sovereign debt market (1921–37): Information efficiency and the impact of rumours](https://doi.org/10.1111/ehr.70150)
  - DOI: 10.1111/ehr.70150
  - Authors: Dan Li; Yajie Wang; Yiran Xie; Zhihao Xu
  - Affiliations: School of Economics Fudan University Shanghai China; International Business School Zhejiang Guangsha Vocational and Technical University of Construction Jinhua Zhejiang China; School of Economics University of Sydney Sydney Australia; Institute of Economics, School of Social Sciences Tsinghua University Beijing China
  - Published: 2026-10-12
  - Crossref journal: The Economic History Review
  - Abstract:
    Abstract China's sovereign debt market emerged with the establishment of the Shanghai Chinese Merchant Stock Exchange (SCMSE) in 1921 during the Beijing Era (1912–27) and flourished in the Prewar Nanjing Era (1927–37), when it dominated the securities market. Despite this growth, our analysis of weekly SCMSE–traded debt returns shows that the market lacked even weak form efficiency throughout the period. This inefficiency fostered a rumour‐driven environment. Using Shenbao , we find that weekly rumours related to the debt market rose from 0.6 in the Beijing Era to 1.5 in the Nanjing Era, yet the Beijing market was more vulnerable to their influence. Positive rumours were ignored in both periods, whilst each negative rumour reduced weekly returns by 96 basis points in the Beijing Era, compared with only 53 basis points in the Nanjing Era. Both markets responded similarly to rumours later verified, yet the Nanjing market was far less unsettled by false reports. Taken together, the evidence suggests that by the Nanjing Era the sovereign debt market had developed greater resilience in processing information, marking a step towards improved efficiency.

  - Published: 2026-10-12

  - Abstract:
    Abstract Using proprietary data on over one million UK Small and Medium‐Sized Enterprises (SMEs) from 2017 to 2024, we examine how ownership composition and director diversity condition the impact of government‐backed Covid‐19 loan programmes on normal (non‐Covid) loan default outcomes. Building on the Behavioural Theory of the Firm and the Attention‐Based View, we develop Attention‐Based Governance (ABG) as a conceptual lens for explaining how SMEs deploy government‐backed slack during systemic crises. Our results show that SMEs with multiple owners from the same family are more effective than single‐owner SMEs in managing Covid support, indicating stronger internal collective oversight and discipline. Furthermore, SMEs with domestic institutional owners and female directors exhibit stronger capability and discipline, while those with foreign institutional owners or foreign directors display higher default risk on normal loans. Additional analysis reveals that foreign‐owned firms are least likely to default on the government‐backed Covid loans themselves. This finding reflects the selective allocation of managerial attention towards highly visible, publicly guaranteed debt to protect reputational capital with global stakeholders. Overall, our study shows how ownership composition and director diversity direct managerial attention and determine whether public funding leads to stronger financial resilience or heightened default risk at the firm level.

  - Published: 2026-10-11
  - Crossref journal: Sociology
  - Abstract:
    Victims’ participation in online investment frauds is not just justified by egoist desire or greed, but also changing relational frames under uncertainty and ambiguity. Drawing on the concept of relational work from economic sociology, this paper examines how victims of the ‘China–Africa Mining Corporation’ fraud justify and sustain their participation in such a crime of interaction. Based on two years of digital fieldwork, we trace victims’ in situ experiences across three stages of participation. First, victims overcame the uncertainty of investing by drawing on personal relationships and state-sanctioned narratives to construct trust. Second, those who became referrers framed recruitment as an act of care, staking their own credibility on the scheme’s legitimacy. Third, as returns failed to materialise, victims sustained belief through practices of patience, loyalty, and moral affirmation. The paper extends the application of relational work theory to cybercrime and demonstrates how victims’ relational work rendered participation meaningful and defensible.

- [The conversion of capital across fields: Hysteresis and the integration paradox among British Muslims](https://doi.org/10.1177/00380385261487060)
  - DOI: 10.1177/00380385261487060
  - Authors: Tahir Abbas
  - Affiliations: Aston University, Birmingham, UK; Centre on Radicalisation, Inclusion and Social Equity, Birmgham, UK
  - Published: 2026-10-11
  - Crossref journal: Sociology
  - Abstract:
    The integration paradox – heightened exclusion among the most socio-economically integrated members of some, though not all, minority communities – challenges linear models of incorporation. This article reconceptualises the paradox through a Bourdieusian field-analytic lens, using latent profile analysis of UK survey data (N = 2535, including 109 Muslim respondents) to identify four configurations of belonging: Adaptive Mainstreamers, Empowered Pluralism, Individualised Assimilation and Double Alienation. British Muslims attain the capital-rich Empowered Pluralism configuration at least as often as others, yet report humiliation at nearly three times the odds of non-Muslims in the same configurations – a misrecognition gap concentrated among Adaptive Mainstreamers and absent within Empowered Pluralism, suggesting strongly resourced dual belonging operates protectively. Employment predicts capital-rich belonging only among respondents below their mid-fifties, consistent with hysteresis in capital convertibility. Heterogeneity analyses confine Double Alienation to foreign-born Muslims. The findings relocate the paradox from average attitudes to misrecognition at equal capital.

    The sociology of law has not yet taken stock of the crucial role played by the admission of guilt in summary justice: it allows criminal law to absolve itself from the violence it employs to manufacture criminals and produce “just” punishments.
