- [Mitigating Market Incompleteness with Minor Market Distortions: The Case of Negative Spot Prices for Electricity](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2025.1854)
  - DOI: 10.1287/opre.2025.1854
  - Authors: Ibrahim Abada; Andreas Ehrenmann
  - Affiliations: Grenoble Ecole de Management, 38000 Grenoble, France; ENGIE, 1000 Brussels, Belgium
  - Published: 2026-09-09
  - Crossref journal: Operations Research
  - Abstract:
    How should policymakers mitigate investment risk in renewable electricity markets without undermining the efficiency of spot markets? Risk mitigation instruments can foster investment in financially incomplete markets but may distort operating incentives, contributing, for example, to increasingly frequent negative electricity prices. In Mitigating Market Incompleteness with Minor Market Distortions: The Case of Negative Spot Prices for Electricity, Ibrahim Abada and Andreas Ehrenmann develop a bi-level programming framework that explicitly incorporates these distortions when addressing market incompleteness. At the lower level, risk-averse agents make investment and operating decisions in an incomplete electricity market. At the upper level, a central planner optimally designs contracts for difference and price markups to maximize social welfare. The paper establishes an existence result, develops an algorithm for finding a solution, and applies the framework to a detailed numerical simulation inspired by the French power system. The results show that carefully designed risk mitigation instruments can enhance welfare while substantially limiting market distortions and the prevalence of negative prices.

  - Published: 2026-09-09
  - Crossref journal: Operations Research
  - Abstract:
    Increasing Parents’ Engagement on EdTech Platforms: Evidence from the Field Simple, well-designed digital nudges can meaningfully increase parents’ participation in early childhood education, according to a large-scale study conducted with Rocket Learning in India. In “Increasing Parents’ Engagement on EdTech Platforms: Evidence from the Field,” Goyal, Okuno, Singhvi, and Singhvi examine peer- and self-comparison messages delivered through digital educational communities. In a randomized controlled trial involving nearly 154,000 parents, peer-comparison nudges increased the average number of parents active each day by 13.5%, whereas self-comparison nudges increased it by 8.0% relative to no nudge. Building on these insights, they develop a data-driven targeting policy that selects the nudge best suited to each parent. A second trial involving 122,000 parents shows that, whereas the targeting policy performs comparably to the best uniform policy on average, it identifies a substantial subgroup—8.8% of parents—for whom targeted nudging increases engagement by 5.4% over the uniform approach. The findings offer nonprofits and policymakers a scalable, low-cost tool for strengthening parental engagement, highlighting both the potential and limitations of personalized nudging.

  - DOI: 10.1111/puar.70186
  - Authors: Michael W. Bauer; Daniel Kovarek; Bárbara Motta; Andrzej Schultz
  - Affiliations: Florence School of Transnational Governance European University Institute Fiesole Italy; Universidade Federal de Sergipe São Cristóvão Brazil; Department of Social and Political Sciences European University Institute Fiesole Italy; Department of Law European University Institute Fiesole Italy
  - Published: 2026-09-10
  - Crossref journal: Public Administration Review
  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT While scholarship has documented how populist and authoritarian‐leaning executives deform bureaucratic institutions, post‐illiberal reconstruction has only begun to receive attention. This paper develops an exploratory framework distinguishing four repair strategies (neutralizing, rectifying, balancing, and democratic hardening) and two repair modes (formal‐institutional and normative‐capacity repair). Applying the heuristic to Poland, Brazil, and Hungary, we posit that the selection and limits of repair strategies follow the gap between the depth of illiberal entrenchment and the legal‐political capacity of successor governments to overcome veto players and enact rectifying reforms. Within the periods observed, formal‐institutional repair has outpaced the reconstruction of professional norms, expertise, and trust, and repair has produced layered administrative orders in which illiberal‐era instruments partly persist and thus remain available to future governments. Administrative repair is thus structurally asymmetric. Democratic successors can rebuild the administrative state, but they are unlikely to restore it to the status quo ante.

- [Impacts of coal smoke on infant mortality: Evidence from industrializing Japan](https://doi.org/10.1111/ehr.70154)
  - DOI: 10.1111/ehr.70154
  - Authors: Tatsuki Inoue
  - Affiliations: School of Commerce Meiji University Tokyo Japan
  - Published: 2026-09-09
  - Crossref journal: The Economic History Review
  - Abstract:
    Abstract This study investigates the health consequences of coal smoke during Japan's early industrialization. Using fixed‐effects models and a prefecture‐level panel dataset on actual coal consumption and infant mortality from 1899 to 1910, this study finds that higher coal consumption was positively associated with infant mortality. The baseline estimate suggests that a one‐standard‐deviation increase in the log of coal consumption was associated with approximately 2.1 additional infant deaths per 1000 live births, and that the growth in coal use was associated with an increase in infant mortality equivalent to approximately 11.8 per cent of the overall rise in infant mortality during the sample period. Further estimates using infant deaths by age interval show that this association was weak during the neonatal period but became more pronounced during the post‐neonatal period, particularly from 1 month to under 6 months after birth. These findings suggest that coal‐based industrialization carried health costs in a non‐Western industrializing economy and that the timing of pollution‐related infant mortality differed from that observed in modern contexts.

  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT Global crises and persistent uncertainty have exposed the vulnerability of supply chains. Procurement departments, traditionally focused on cost optimization, are increasingly required to act as strategic orchestrators of supply chains. This paper examines a data‐driven transformation within a European automotive procurement department, revealing how contextual frictions and organizational realities shape transformation outcomes. Based on a qualitative case study, our findings show that poor internal data quality, limited access to external supply chain data, multiple operational challenges from recurring disruptions, shortages or supplier insolvencies and insufficient institutionalization of data‐driven activities critically impede the value realization of data‐driven transformations. Building on these findings, we propose six actionable recommendations that address the identified barriers and position procurement as both a catalyst for effective data management and a strategic enabler of data‐driven supply chains. Key best practices include structured data negotiation with suppliers, embedded qualification programs and sustained leadership engagement to foster a data‐driven mindset. The study contributes to information systems research by advancing a domain‐specific understanding of data‐driven transformation and highlighting procurement as a strategic enabler of intelligent, data‐based decision‐making under conditions of crisis and uncertainty. Our findings provide actionable guidance for procurement practitioners and their data‐driven activities.

  - DOI: 10.1093/jopart/muag029
  - Authors: Adalmir de Oliveira Gomes
  - Affiliations: University of Brasilia (UnB), Colina, Block H, Apartment 205, Darcy Ribeiro University Campus, Asa Norte , Brasília, Federal District, Brazil, Zip Code: 70904-108
  - Published: 2026-09-09
  - Crossref journal: Journal of Public Administration Research and Theory
  - Abstract:
    Abstract This article asks a central question in digital government research: why do local governments operating under similar institutional conditions follow sharply different digital transformation trajectories over time? Drawing on theories of gradual institutional change, the study conceptualizes digital transformation not as a discrete adoption outcome, but as a process of institutional conversion through which technological capacity is translated into sustained digital service provision. Information governance is theorized as the key conditioning mechanism linking technological resources to durable organizational outcomes. The analysis combines longitudinal panel models, fixed-effects estimators, and trajectory-based clustering using a panel of all Brazilian municipalities (MUNIC/IBGE; N = 5,569) across seven waves between 2004 and 2024. Results show that increases in technological capacity are associated with subsequent gains in digital services, but these effects are gradual and conditional. Information governance significantly shapes how municipalities mobilize and stabilize technological resources over time, while interaction models reveal non-linear and diminishing returns consistent with institutional coordination limits. Trajectory analyses identify four distinct patterns of digital transformation, ranging from ‘institutionalized pioneers’ to ‘persistent stagnation’, demonstrating that digital development unfolds through path-dependent and institutionally anchored trajectories rather than through simple convergence. The article contributes to public administration research in three ways: provides rare longitudinal evidence on local digital transformation over two decades; integrates institutional change theory into digital government research by conceptualizing governance as a mechanism of institutional conversion; and advances a trajectory-based explanation of digital transformation that shifts attention from episodic technological adoption to cumulative institutional processes shaping long-run administrative performance.
