- [The Chains That Bind? Restrictive Covenants and Labor Market Entrants' Perceived Occupational Mobility](https://doi.org/10.1111/irel.70037)
  - DOI: 10.1111/irel.70037
  - Authors: Vincent Jerald Ramos; Benjamin Radoc
  - Affiliations: University of Southampton and Hertie School Berlin Southampton Hampshire UK; Bangko Sentral Ng Pilipinas and Ateneo de Manila University Manila Philippines
  - Published: 2026-07-27
  - Crossref journal: Industrial Relations: A Journal of Economy and Society
  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT Using a conjoint experiment on highly educated labor market entrants in the Philippines, we examine how restrictive covenants shape perceived occupational mobility. Respondents compared hypothetical jobseeker profiles with randomized non‐compete, training repayment, and non‐solicitation clauses, then chose the likely applicant to four mobility pathways: vertical or lateral jobs within or across industries. Findings show that non‐compete clauses deter within‐industry and encourage across‐industry perceived mobility. Training repayment and non‐solicitation clauses exert targeted effects depending on scope conditions. Finally, these patterns hold across contractual breadth and firms' monitoring stringency, and are attenuated by positive beliefs about loyalty norms and perceived sufficiency of legal recourse.

- [Sex and the City: Demographics, Spatial Sorting, and the Marriage Market](https://doi.org/10.1111/iere.70090)
  - DOI: 10.1111/iere.70090
  - Authors: Min Fang; Zibin Huang; Yushi Wang; Yu (Alan) Yang
  - Affiliations: Department of Economics and Decision Sciences China Europe International Business School Pudong Shanghai China; Lanzillotti Public Policy Research Center University of Florida Gainesville Florida USA; College of Business Shanghai University of Finance and Economics Shanghai China; Shanghai Institute of International Finance and Economics Shanghai China; International Business School Hainan University Haikou China; Guanghua School of Management Peking University Beijing China
  - Published: 2026-07-27
  - Crossref journal: International Economic Review
  - Abstract:
    ABSTRACT We study how the interaction between demographic changes and spatial sorting affects marriage matching across space. Using data from China, we first present stylized facts on the diverging spatial distribution of singlehood characterized by a high singles rate for females (males) in more (less) developed cities. We then build a quantitative spatial equilibrium model. It reveals that more highly educated women sort into the service sector in more developed cities than men. With persistent social norms of hypergamy, this results in more failed marriage matches for females (males) in more (less) developed cities, thereby lowering the national marriage rate.
